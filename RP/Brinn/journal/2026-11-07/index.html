Sister Brinn Sarjeant · 7 November · Year 25 ADP

Hall of Thanes

There are days when a priest learns more about healing in an hour than she has learned in all her years of study.

Beneath the Mountain

The sleeping bag had been worth the journey.

Brinn had followed sixteen weary adventurers to Thoradin's Wall the previous night, through forests, marshes, mountains, and more danger than any reasonable person ought to encounter while searching for somewhere comfortable to sleep. Yet when morning came, she could hardly complain. She had her reward, and the achievement had left her with something else: confidence.

Today she meant to put that confidence to work.

She had learned a stronger healing prayer, and there was talk of trouble beneath Ironforge. Dark Iron dwarves had entered the old halls beneath the mountain, disturbing places that should have remained undisturbed.

She had come to Azeroth's roads to help people, not to become a soldier. Nevertheless, people seemed remarkably determined to require healing in the most dangerous places imaginable.

And so she prepared for her first great test as a party healer.

In Ironforge, she found a company assembling for the Hall of Thanes.

Sarah Connur, a young warrior, took charge. Beside her stood Mith Lightscar, a paladin whose devotion to the Light was reassuring in a place rumored to be filled with restless spirits. Clarence Liongard was a mage, ready with spells and conjured water, while Ariadryn Brackston brought her bow and hunting companion.

Brinn would be their priest.

Clarence offered to conjure water.

"I can use some," Brinn replied.

"I'll have it ready for you."

That was a good beginning. A healer who had no water was much like a cook without a fire.

While they assembled, the conversation turned to more personal matters.

Sarah spoke of her father, who had disappeared on an expedition to Blackrock Mountain the year before. He had fought in the Second War alongside Sir Mith, and Sarah hoped someday to discover what had become of him.

Brinn listened.

"It is a dangerous place," she said.

She knew what it meant to have an unanswered question about a father. There was no comfort in offering certainty where none existed.

Ariadryn admitted that Ironforge was the farthest she had ever traveled from Westfall.

Sarah admired the grandeur of the city. Ariadryn agreed, although she thought it rather heavy on the sulfur.

Brinn had another reason for coming.

"Someone told me the dwarves need help."

It was enough.

Before the company departed, she turned to Sarah.

"Sarah, do you like healers?"

"You would be my first," the warrior answered.

"Be gentle."

There was laughter enough in that exchange to take the edge off the gathering apprehension.

Sarah, however, offered something more valuable than reassurance.

Her father had taught her to make sure a healer's mana was full before advancing into danger.

"Thank you, my friend," Brinn replied.

It was a small courtesy, but she would remember it long after the dungeon was cleared.

The Restless Dead

The passage lay beneath the High Seat, beyond the king's throne room and down a shadowed corridor.

Ironforge above was alive with hammering, furnace heat, voices, and commerce. Below, the sounds seemed swallowed by the stone.

The Hall of Thanes was no ordinary cavern.

It was a place of remembrance, where generations of dwarven dead had been laid to rest. Ancient stonework preserved the memory of rulers and warriors, while the air itself seemed burdened by grief and resentment.

Someone had violated that peace.

The first spirits appeared as they entered the burial chambers.

Tormented Souls drifted through the gloom. Enraged Apparitions turned upon the living as though the company itself were responsible for their suffering.

Mith sensed the darkness.

"I sense much evil in this place," he warned.

Brinn felt an uncomfortable stirring of recognition.

She knew something of the power that answered suffering with suffering. There were prayers she could speak that harmed rather than healed. She had used such power when circumstances demanded it.

But today she had come to keep people alive.

Sarah advanced, and the first apparition rushed toward her.

Brinn raised her hands.

A prayer of healing settled upon the warrior, followed by the protective force of a shield. Another spell followed as an injury opened, then another as the fight spread through the chamber.

The company worked together, but the dead did not surrender easily.

Again and again, Brinn found herself drawing the attention of enemies while trying to protect her companions. An apparition would turn from Sarah. A restless spirit would move toward the priest. Her healing was necessary, but it made her a target.

There was little opportunity for reflection.

Shield. Renew. Heal.

Watch Sarah.

Watch Mith.

Watch everyone.

And watch the shadows behind her.

"Wait," she called as the others moved forward.

Her reserves of mana were already dwindling.

The party paused long enough for her to drink.

Then they moved again.

An Ancient Grudge

Among the tombs, a ghostly attendant remained near the resting place of Faldrim Anvilmar.

The old thane was trapped by a grievance that had outlasted his mortal life. Whatever wrong had first consumed him, it had become a prison.

Brinn found the thought profoundly sad.

Death ought to have ended such suffering.

Sarah studied the chamber.

"Clear the room," she ordered. "I have a feeling Faldrim will be quite the challenge."

Mith was resolute.

"He will fall under our combined might."

Brinn would have preferred to think they were granting him peace rather than defeating him, but she understood the necessity.

They cleared the surrounding apparitions, rested, and prepared.

Then Faldrim stirred.

"Defilers! Miscreants! Leave this place!"

His voice echoed through the burial chamber, filled with the rage of a man who had forgotten the difference between his enemies and those who might release him.

The battle began.

Sarah met the furious spirit while Clarence and Ariadryn attacked from a distance. Mith stood with them, offering the protection of his faith and his weapon.

Brinn kept her attention on the wounded.

Faldrim's fury was dangerous, but it was not endless.

When the final blow came, the old thane fell silent.

For a moment Brinn stood still, listening to the quiet left behind.

She hoped it was peace.

The company discovered an Ephemeral Choker among the relics of the encounter, and Brinn received it. She put it on with some satisfaction, knowing it would help her carry out her responsibilities.

Sarah laughed.

"Brinn, that'll help with my recklessness."

Brinn suspected no necklace in the world was powerful enough to accomplish that completely.

Not long afterward, Sarah found herself afflicted by a curse that none of them could remove.

"Guess I'll be a statue for the time being," she joked.

Brinn could heal wounds and diseases. Curses were another matter.

It troubled her to discover the limits of her knowledge while someone was depending upon her.

There was only one remedy for that sort of ignorance.

She would have to learn more.

Fire Beneath the Stone

The first part of the halls had been filled with ghosts.

Deeper within, the company encountered the living consequences of the Dark Iron intrusion.

Dark Iron Looters prowled the passages with their hounds. Engineers and summoners worked among strange mechanisms and elemental forces. The halls had become a place of plunder rather than remembrance.

Sarah pushed forward, occasionally calling a halt for Brinn's mana.

"Brinn, have a drink."

Brinn was grateful, although she sometimes wondered whether she would spend more time drinking water than performing her actual duties.

The enemies struck hard.

She cast healing prayers until the words seemed to run together. When an ally's wounds worsened, she drew upon stronger healing. When she could anticipate danger, she raised a shield before the blow fell.

Disease could be cured.

Burns and cuts could be healed.

Exhaustion, unfortunately, required something rather more ordinary.

Water.

At one point Sarah called for another pause.

"Mana. For Brinn."

Then, with a confidence Brinn was beginning to trust, Sarah announced that they were doing well.

Ahead lay a chamber of fire.

The company prepared to confront Magmatus, a great elemental force of living flame.

Sarah gave Ariadryn instructions to send her companion against the summoner while she handled the principal threat.

Mith offered a quiet assurance.

"The Light will always provide."

Brinn hoped it would provide quickly.

"Flame consumes all!" roared Magmatus.

Heat washed through the chamber.

For Brinn, the battle became a series of urgent decisions. Which companion was most injured? Who could endure another strike? Who needed protection immediately? Could she spare enough mana for a stronger prayer, or would the next wound demand it?

She had learned that healing was not merely the act of casting a spell.

It was judgment.

It was knowing when to act and when to conserve strength.

Above all, it was accepting responsibility for people who might be unaware of how close they had come to disaster.

The elemental was eventually extinguished.

The company survived.

Brinn drank more water.

The Mechanical Guardian

Beyond the fiery chamber lay further evidence of the Dark Iron dwarves' intrusion.

Stone golems stood among the tombs, while machinery of unfamiliar construction guarded the stolen treasures.

Sarah examined a particularly large mechanical creature.

"That robot..." she murmured.

Something about it reminded her of another machine, though she could not immediately place the memory.

Soon they stood before the guardian known as Plunder.

The mechanism stirred.

"Loot acquisition protocol engaged."

Brinn stared.

She had encountered bandits, wolves, gnolls, and furious ghosts. None had ever announced its intention to steal from her with such remarkable formality.

Plunder attacked.

The company fought among stone and metal as the guardian struck with mechanical strength. Sarah had already warned that enemies in these chambers could knock adventurers backward, sometimes into further danger.

Brinn held her ground where she could, shielding her companions and restoring their wounds as the battle unfolded.

There was no great speech at the end.

The machine simply stopped.

For a moment the hall was quiet.

Brinn regarded the motionless guardian with relief.

It seemed almost a pity that something so clever had been built for such a foolish purpose.

Then she remembered how much damage it had caused.

She decided she did not pity it very much.

The Greatest Heist in History

The final chambers held the Dark Iron commander.

Durgen Dirgehammer had come into the ancestral halls in search of treasure and power. He had turned the burial place of Ironforge's forebears into the scene of an audacious theft.

Sarah gathered the company before the confrontation.

She instructed Ariadryn to direct her pet against one marked enemy, while the party concentrated upon another.

Only then would they attack Durgen himself.

Brinn appreciated the preparation.

She had learned enough from the day's fighting to know that bravery without discipline was an excellent way to keep a healer busy.

The Dark Iron commander saw them approach.

"Ye think you can stop the greatest heist in history?"

Sarah answered with steel.

The others followed.

Durgen fought fiercely. Lesser golems and Dark Iron servants added to the chaos, and the company had to hold together while the commander tried to break their formation.

Brinn could scarcely spare a glance at the enemy.

Sarah's wounds demanded attention.

Mith needed protection.

There were others to watch, and every spell carried a cost.

She had spent much of her life believing that helping people meant placing herself between them and suffering.

Now, beneath Ironforge, she understood that sometimes it meant standing behind people who were facing the danger for her.

She trusted them to hold the line.

They trusted her to keep them alive.

The fighting seemed to stretch far beyond the few moments it actually occupied.

Then Durgen Dirgehammer fell.

The Dark Iron incursion had lost its commander.

Among the possessions recovered from the chamber were Dwarven Heirlooms and a remarkable treaty, a document concerning the old relationship between the Bronzebeard and Wildhammer clans.

Brinn studied the ancient agreement with interest.

Kingdoms could quarrel for generations. Families could carry grudges long after those who began them were dead. Yet someone had once believed it worth the effort to put peace into writing.

After seeing Faldrim's restless spirit, she found that thought particularly meaningful.

Perhaps the living had a responsibility to settle their differences before they became another burden for the dead.

The company gathered the recovered belongings and prepared to leave.

Clarence discovered the hidden lever that opened the main door.

Before parting, he thanked his companions for their fellowship.

Ariadryn agreed that it had been a job well done.

Brinn turned to Sarah.

"Thank you, Sarah."

"Thank you, Brinn," the warrior answered.

Then she added something Brinn would treasure more than any trinket recovered beneath the mountain.

"You saved my life."

Brinn felt her face warm.

She had come into the Hall of Thanes uncertain whether she was ready to serve as a healer.

Now she had her answer.

Not because she had performed perfectly. She knew how often she had needed to pause, how close her mana had come to exhaustion, and how much she still had to learn.

But Sarah was alive.

So were the others.

That was enough.

A Priest's Reward

Back in Ironforge, Brinn returned the recovered heirlooms, reported what had happened among the restless dead, and delivered word of Durgen's defeat.

The treaty was brought before King Magni Bronzebeard.

Her service earned the gratitude of those who had sent her below the mountain, along with equipment that would make the next journey a little less perilous.

She had gained a cloak, bracers, the Ephemeral Choker, and a wand called Deepblaze.

But the most important reward was harder to carry in a satchel.

She had survived her first dungeon as a healer.

More importantly, she had helped four other adventurers survive it too.

By the time she had finished reporting to the dwarves, she had reached her seventeenth level of experience.

Her laughter escaped before she thought to contain it.

The road ahead was still long.

For the first time, however, she could imagine herself answering the call when a company needed a priest.

The Long Road Back to Westfall

There was little opportunity to rest upon her accomplishment.

Westfall still needed help.

Returning to the familiar fields, Brinn found the same hardships waiting where she had left them.

The Riverpaw gnolls continued to trouble the mines. Defias bandits and smugglers prowled the roads. Harvest golems wandered where farmers should have been working.

Brinn helped clear the mines alongside another adventurer, Ryn Thorne. When their work was finished, they exchanged simple farewells before returning to their separate errands.

Later she joined Nivlac Blackthorn in hunting Defias Pillagers.

The countryside was so thoroughly cleared of enemies in places that they had difficulty finding any remaining.

"Place been damn purged," Nivlac observed.

Brinn could hardly argue.

She was glad for his company. It was easier to make progress when someone else was watching the road.

At Sentinel Hill, the People's Militia had more work for her. There were bandanas to recover, gnolls to deal with, and Defias outlaws to drive away.

Yet the most troubling part of the day involved the Brotherhood itself.

The trail that had begun with ordinary robberies and stolen supplies was becoming something more organized.

Brinn found a mysterious message and followed the evidence. A Defias prisoner, willing to betray his former associates, helped reveal the direction of their hiding place.

Moonbrook.

There, the Brotherhood's influence seemed to reach into every abandoned building and ruined street.

Brinn had once thought of the Defias as little more than desperate bandits.

She no longer believed that explanation was sufficient.

There was leadership behind them.

Planning.

Purpose.

And perhaps something more dangerous waiting beyond the ruined village.

She did not yet know the whole truth of their origins, or what had happened between the Brotherhood and Stormwind's rulers.

But she intended to find out.

The Question of Destructive Magic

The trail carried Brinn back toward Stormwind and further errands involving dangerous devices and explosive materials.

She consulted those who understood such things, learned something of remote detonators and dynamite, and discovered that many people seemed to regard explosions as an entirely reasonable solution to their difficulties.

She was not convinced.

Yet she had seen enough of Westfall to recognize that ordinary methods were failing.

Sometimes an obstacle could not be talked away.

Sometimes a door had to be broken.

The thought was not entirely unlike her relationship with Shadow magic.

She had no desire to abandon the Holy Light. It remained the foundation of her faith and the reason she had become a priest.

Still, she was beginning to understand the difference between refusing to cause harm and refusing to act when harm was necessary to protect others.

She returned to her studies and learned stronger prayers, including improvements to her protective shielding and the darker spell known as Shadow Word: Pain.

She also learned to dispel harmful magic.

That last lesson pleased her immensely.

After Sarah's curse in the Hall of Thanes, she was especially determined not to remain helpless in the face of an affliction she might someday learn to remove.

Her studies and adventures brought her to level eighteen.

It was another milestone, but it did not yet feel as important as the words Sarah had spoken beneath Ironforge.

You saved my life.

Toward the Deadmines

By late afternoon Brinn had returned to Westfall.

She walked among the roads and fields that had become so familiar over the past several days.

The guards were still standing watch. Farmers were still struggling. The Defias Brotherhood remained a threat.

She paused near one of the guards.

"Must be hard work being a guard here," she remarked.

A moment later, considering the evidence of the day's adventures, she added:

"The Brotherhood is everywhere."

There were still questions to answer.

The Defias had established themselves around Moonbrook, and Brinn had learned enough to suspect that their activities reached deeper than the abandoned buildings visible from the road.

Now she had been given explosives intended for a new undertaking.

The Deadmines.

She had faced the restless dead, Dark Iron raiders, a living furnace, and a mechanical guardian beneath Ironforge.

Somewhere under Westfall, another danger waited.

Brinn adjusted the strap of her satchel and looked toward Moonbrook.

She had not forgotten why she first came to Northshire Abbey.

She was a healer.

That was still what she wanted to be.

But she was learning that a healer's road sometimes led through very dark places.

And this time, she would go with her eyes open.

The Holy Light had not promised her an easy road. It had simply given her a reason to walk it.

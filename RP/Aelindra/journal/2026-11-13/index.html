The Wolves of Shadowfang

The day would take Aelindra from the rain-soaked Wetlands to the haunted halls of Shadowfang Keep, and finally into the mountains of Redridge. There would be new friendships, unexpected kindness, dangerous battles, and one particularly humiliating encounter with a wolf.

By day's end, she would be a better hunter, though she had gathered a few bruises along with her experience.

Morning — The Roads Still Untraveled

The morning began with unfinished business.

Aelindra had been traveling across the Eastern Kingdoms with Sylthara, her faithful cat, gathering information about two places increasingly mentioned among adventurers: the Stockades beneath Stormwind and the mysterious excavation in the Wetlands.

There was a frustrating problem. The order of the adventures was unclear, and some of the work seemed intended for someone more experienced.

She asked her guild whether the Stockades should come before the Wetlands expedition. The answers did little to settle the matter. Still, by the time she departed Stormwind, she had collected several assignments connected to the prison: What Comes Around..., Crime and Punishment, The Stockade Riots, Quell the Uprising, and The Color of Blood.

There was something unsettling about the city keeping so many dangerous prisoners beneath its own streets. But the prison could wait. The Wetlands were calling.

At Whelgar's Excavation Site, the dwarves had come seeking knowledge beneath the earth, only to have their work disrupted by aggressive raptors. Aelindra helped with Ormer's investigations, searched for the missing expedition, and took on the task of uncovering the past.

The country itself was an education. Heavy mist hung low over pools and marshes. Ancient dwarven ruins lay scattered across a landscape that had seen wars long before she was born. To a girl raised among floating islands and open skies, this land seemed almost determined to swallow anyone foolish enough to trust it.

She met Harrend Hardholler while attempting to help him through trouble. The attempt proved rather more dangerous than expected.

"It is ok," she called afterward. "I was trying to help you and was way over my head."

Then, with evident concern for her companion, she added, "poor kitty."

Harrend admitted that he had been running toward a quest marker without thinking carefully enough about the danger. Aelindra could hardly condemn the habit. She had done much the same herself.

By midmorning she had helped investigate the excavation, assisted the Greenwarden, fought crocolisks, and reached her twenty-fourth level.

She celebrated with a laugh. Sylthara, perhaps, was less impressed with the increasing number of things her mistress insisted on carrying.

The Wetlands were not finished teaching her humility. Two deaths among the marshes quickly followed her achievement. Nevertheless, she returned to her work, completed the Fire Taboo investigation, and continued toward the coast.

Along the way she explored Saltspray Glen and the remains of the Lost Fleet. These were not merely names upon a map. The sea had left its mark upon both places: salt spray carried inland by the wind, and wrecked vessels resting beneath the waters of Baradin Bay.

Later, her travels took her back toward more familiar country. She acquired a costly fishing book, learned more about her trade, and ventured into Duskwood, where she learned a stronger Bite technique for Sylthara.

The cat was becoming a formidable hunter in her own right.

But the day's greatest adventure had yet to begin.

Shadowfang Keep — The Wolves of Silverpine

The afternoon expedition, 12:38–1:45 p.m.

The invitation to Shadowfang Keep promised adventure, companionship, and a chance to see a place Aelindra had never visited.

Her companions were Tess Reitveld, Grannie Goodness, Vern Wallston, and Rel Puresilver. They were a cheerful company, apparently as fond of conversation as battle.

Aelindra was still making her way toward the keep when she announced, "I am flying in."

Others were traveling by road or boat. There was much discussion about who had reached Southshore, who could summon whom, and who needed provisions.

"I hope I am not keeping you," Aelindra said.

Nobody seemed particularly concerned. Grannie was busy lamenting the prospect of making the entire journey only to be summoned at the very end.

As they assembled, the conversation turned to food, water, healthstones, and the importance of remaining properly hydrated. Grannie seemed especially enthusiastic about tea.

It was a reassuring beginning.

The journey took Aelindra through Hillsbrad, where green pastures and rolling hills still bore some resemblance to the lost northern kingdom. But Silverpine was different.

The trees there stood pale beneath a gray sky. The land had suffered terribly during the fall of Lordaeron. Abandoned farms and ruined settlements bore witness to the Scourge's passage.

Above Pyrewood Village, Shadowfang Keep rose from its rocky bluff.

Once the stronghold of Baron Silverlaine, it had become the domain of Archmage Arugal, whose terrible experiments with worgen had brought misery to the surrounding countryside.

Aelindra had never seen the fortress before.

"How safe is this road?" she asked as the party approached.

Shortly afterward came a more pressing question.

"I have never been here, where is the entrance"

They found it.

And went inside.

Within the haunted halls

Shadowfang Keep was unlike the dwarven halls and mines Aelindra had explored earlier in her travels.

The stonework was ancient, the corridors narrow, and the atmosphere oppressive. Ghostly servants, restless guardsmen, savage wolves, and creatures twisted by dark magic inhabited what had once been a noble residence.

Aelindra kept her firearm ready while Sylthara moved ahead alongside the party's front line.

They first encountered the imprisoned Rethilgore and the haunted guardians of the lower keep. The fighting carried them past Razorclaw the Butcher and through chambers where the dead seemed unwilling to remain dead.

The party made steady progress.

Grannie complained about the creatures' disgusting habits and their apparent refusal to cooperate with her Shadow magic. Tess suggested that perhaps the undead were simply prejudiced against older women.

Even Aelindra found herself smiling.

Then came Baron Silverlaine.

Whatever dignity the old lord had possessed in life, death had robbed him of peace. The company confronted his restless spirit and defeated him, passing deeper into the cursed fortress.

Commander Springvale fell next, followed by Odo the Blindwatcher.

As the fighting continued, Aelindra learned that danger within the keep was not always a matter of how hard a creature struck. The worst threats were often the narrow passages, the unexpected groups of enemies, and the difficulty of keeping everyone together.

Grannie occasionally found herself spending more effort fighting than healing.

The others joked about it afterward.

For all their foolish conversation, they were proving an effective company.

The wolves grow bolder

The upper halls brought increasingly savage opposition.

Shadowfang Rageteeth prowled the corridors. Sons of Arugal attacked with vicious strength. The party fought its way through them, sometimes emerging from encounters with rather less health than anyone would have preferred.

Tess found herself making frequent use of her curse-removing magic.

Grannie nearly exhausted her mana.

Vern occasionally relied a little too enthusiastically upon his own life force to power his spells.

At one point, after a particularly dangerous exchange, Grannie demanded to know what Vern had been thinking.

He admitted that he had used Life Tap a few too many times.

Aelindra could only advise, "Don't cry."

Sylthara, meanwhile, remained her trusted companion. But navigating the keep with a large hunting cat was a challenge. Narrow corners, crowded passages, and sudden changes in direction all required attention.

At one point Aelindra dismissed her cat to negotiate the route. The separation would become another reminder that a hunter must think carefully about where her companion can follow.

The party pressed onward.

Fenrus the Devourer was defeated, along with the sinister creatures haunting the corridors beyond.

They confronted Wolf Master Nandos and his savage beasts.

And finally they came to Archmage Arugal.

The fall of Arugal

The master of Shadowfang Keep was no ordinary enemy.

His command of dangerous magic and the unnatural creatures surrounding him had turned the fortress into a monument to his own disastrous ambitions.

For Aelindra, who came from a people whose homeland had nearly been destroyed by the failure of elemental powers, there was something particularly troubling about a mage convinced that he could control forces greater than himself.

She fought beside her companions as they faced Arugal and his guardians.

The battle was fierce, but the company prevailed.

Archmage Arugal fell.

For a moment, the terrible keep seemed almost peaceful.

The party could scarcely believe that the expedition was finished.

"That is It," Aelindra confirmed.

Grannie expressed surprise that the adventure had been shorter than some of the other dungeons she had visited.

Everyone exchanged thanks and congratulations.

Aelindra added her own.

"Yes, thank you."

It had been a successful expedition.

But the adventure was not quite over.

The wolf they left behind

There is a particular danger in believing that the fighting has ended.

Aelindra was still making her way out of the keep while her companions were already discussing their next destinations.

They had fought through its most dangerous chambers, defeated Arugal, and survived everything the fortress had placed before them.

Surely the journey out would be simple.

It was not.

In the corridors behind the departing company, a Shadowfang Wolfguard and its accompanying worg remained very much alive.

Aelindra found herself facing them without the protection of the party's front line.

She attempted to escape.

The worg struck, and its attack left her dazed. Suddenly every step became a struggle. The Wolfguard closed upon her as the smaller beast continued biting.

The blows came in rapid succession.

There was no time to gather everyone back together. No opportunity to establish a safe position for Sylthara. The wolfguard's attacks and the worg's teeth overwhelmed her.

Aelindra fell upon the stone floor.

Her companions had already been exchanging farewells, unaware of how dangerous the return journey had become.

When she finally found words for the disaster, they were only two.

"bad wolf"

It was not the glorious ending she had imagined.

Even after recovering and attempting to return through the keep, she suffered another death before finally leaving the cursed fortress behind.

If the day had taught her anything, it was that the last enemy encountered on the way out could be every bit as deadly as the master of the dungeon.

Aelindra had survived Arugal.

She had not survived the journey home quite so gracefully.

An Unexpected Return to Southshore

Even the road back to safety held another nasty surprise.

After escaping Shadowfang, Aelindra found herself in Southshore, where the dead appeared to have further business with her. Near the cemetery, Helcular's sinister puppets attacked.

Once again, she was overwhelmed.

An adventurer might reasonably expect an afternoon's worth of fighting undead and werewolves to be enough, but apparently the spirits of Hillsbrad disagreed.

This time there was no dark corridor to blame. She had been caught in the open, with Southshore's buildings visible beyond the graveyard.

The incident might have discouraged another hunter. Aelindra had already faced worse.

She recovered, gathered herself, and continued.

Afternoon — An Adventure Among the Blackrock Orcs

Later that afternoon, Aelindra turned her attention toward Redridge Mountains.

The wooded hills were a welcome change from Silverpine's haunted ruins, though they had dangers of their own. Blackrock orcs occupied camps and strongholds near the passage into the Burning Steppes.

Aelindra had work to do, including the continuing campaign against the Blackrock forces.

At Render's Camp and Render's Rock, she found another opportunity to work with fellow adventurers.

Ranya Sunwisp and Thorsten Steelbeard became her companions. Thorsten had been having difficulty with the dangers and the strange business of being separated onto different layers of the world.

He asked to join them, admitting that he could not face the orcs alone.

Aelindra was more than happy to welcome him.

"get the dwarf too," she said.

Soon the little company was exchanging information about their assignments, sharing quests, and negotiating their way through the occupied territory.

Ranya admitted that she could scarcely remember the route, having been away from the place for years.

Aelindra understood perfectly.

There were Blackrock champions to defeat and a missing soldier to find.

They discovered Corporal Keeshan, a prisoner of the orcs, and helped escort him back toward safety.

It was exactly the sort of adventure Aelindra enjoyed most: strangers meeting upon the road, discovering common purpose, and helping one another through danger.

By the time the corporal had reached safety, Aelindra had earned her twenty-fifth level.

She thanked her companions warmly.

"I am Aelindra, thank you again for the adventure"

And shortly afterward:

"I will take my leave, my thanks again. Let us take wing."

It was a far happier conclusion than her earlier attempts to escape Shadowfang Keep.

Evening — Learning, Resting, and Remembering

With her adventures complete, Aelindra attended to the practical work that every good hunter eventually faces.

Sylthara had earned additional training. Her mistress learned stronger techniques to improve the cat's stamina and natural defenses.

Aelindra also devoted attention to First Aid, learning to prepare better bandages and useful medicines.

There was something reassuring about such ordinary work after an afternoon spent among the dead.

The young Skyborne had begun her travels believing that the world below the clouds would be full of mysteries.

She had been correct.

But its greatest surprises were not necessarily ancient ruins or terrible monsters.

Sometimes they were the kindness of strangers, the cheerful absurdity of a dungeon party, or the willingness of two unfamiliar adventurers to help rescue a missing soldier.

And sometimes they were a particularly unpleasant wolf.

As evening approached, Aelindra held her hearthstone in her hand.

"Back tomorrow. Tell them to wait."

Sylthara would be ready.

And somewhere beyond the next hill, another adventure was already waiting for them.

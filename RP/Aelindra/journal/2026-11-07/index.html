What the Wind Still Held

Before the first step, there was a young elf with a bow.

Pale skin, light-blue eyes, and fair hair arranged with more care than a morning on the cliffs would repay. Blue Gale marks ran across her face, and the Zephyr tattoos carried the pattern farther down. She tucked a feather into her hair and fastened her mother's pearls. The gold sat close enough to her gear that a bowstring couldn't catch on it. High Order. Hunter. Aelindra Ser'jaen'thal.

Then the view widened, and there was Zephras.

Long before Aelindra was born, Highborne exiles fled Eldre'Thalas and made a bargain with the spirits of the air. Those spirits raised a piece of land into Skywall, and the exiles built a home on it. The Shen'dorei had lived above the clouds ever since. Now the spirits were gone. Whole settlements were coming apart, and the refugees who made it to Zephras brought accounts of islands that had once seemed as permanent as this one. The Windshapers sought to mend the old bond. The High Order searched its ancestral knowledge for a way to keep the isle aloft. The Al'Aketh had put their faith in Al'Akir.

Aelindra had spent enough mornings at the edge of a terrace to know what a healthy pylon sounded like. She listened for that sound now, before she turned to answer Ailee Farheart.

Thendal

Ailee sent her to Rorian the Dayseeker for her coming of age. Rorian set her to thinning the vuldren juveniles, and Elatrell Featherlight had an infestation of cirrusflies for her to look into. It was work a young hunter could do with a bow and a little patience. Patience proved the scarcer of the two. A cirrusfly could change direction between the instant she loosed and the instant the arrow reached it. By the time she went to see Tai'ree Farsight with Rorian's scribbled note, she was ready to listen to anyone who could tell her where a creature intended to be.

Tai'ree taught her to track beasts. Halaan Hawk-Eye lent her his sight so she could study the distant anchor pylon. She had seen it a thousand times from the grove. The close view made its uneven magic harder to dismiss. Then Myriaal Mistwake sent her up a watchtower to practice Walk on Air. She had used it before, but there was always a moment when she stepped past the last bit of stone and her stomach expected an argument. Her descent slowed. She landed with her pearls still swinging.

At the standing stones, a broken ley line had brought magic close enough to the surface for Falorne Fallwind to send her out to read it. Yala Windwatcher had a more immediate problem. The Al'Aketh were agitating the local spirits, and there were converts and Roiling Winds to deal with before anyone could study the place in peace. Aelindra worked between the stones and the grove, gathering windstone clusters where she could. Some of the stones she tried were already spent. She took a moment to eat with the clouds passing beneath her, then went back to the search.

When it came time to face the Cirrusfly Queen, Neranduil Zephyrgale joined her. A creature that big ought to have been easier to hit. It turned out to be much easier with a second person there. They brought the queen down, and Aelindra went back to finish her report on the ley line and carry Yala's news to Rorian. By then Tai'ree had shown her how to put venom behind a shot and keep her feet when something got close enough to make the bow awkward.

Rorian sent her on to Aetheen of the Gales. Somewhere in the business of returning to Thendal and setting out again, word came that Mid Life Aggro had accepted her into the guild. She sent back a brief thanks. There were still windstones to gather, and the ursera had come too close to the grove.

She finished delivering the windstones and claws and took her first lessons in skinning and tanning. She had also gone into Thendal Cave after the scavengers and their den mother, Urs'anah, and brought word of it to Aetheen. There was time enough for one more job for Thendal. Hanaa Nightwind needed help with Al'Aketh thugs, among them Malduko Cloudcrush. By the time Aelindra turned toward Shen'dar, she had firsthand news of what the cult was doing around the grove.

Shen'dar

Aetheen sent her to Constable Aonda with a word of confidence in the woman. On the way, Aelindra stopped at Raan Wildwind's campfire. Sitting down was the whole lesson, though she kept finding reasons to get up until the warmth made its case. In the village, Zerril Softbreeze taught her the beginnings of camp cooking. She could make a fire, roast boar, and turn an egg and a pinch of herbs into supper. Later, Mendalass Tattermend showed her how to make a camp chair. After that morning, she was prepared to call a dry seat a piece of useful equipment.

She sat with Rathiril Sunlance and heard the High Order's case for recovering the arcane knowledge of their forebears. She knew the argument from her parents' table. Hearing it in a village where people were missing supplies and posting notices about dangerous beasts gave it a different weight. Rathiril sent her against Windshaper novice seers in the highlands. She had seen Windshapers keep watch at sacred stones. She would have liked to understand how it had come to this. By the time she had an answer worth giving, she suspected there would be another day's work in it.

Aonda told her to ask around, and there was plenty to ask about. Hippogryphs were harassing the village. Taleen Shimmerthread wanted their down. Indari Sunseam needed Prideclaw pelts, the larder needed eggs and strider meat, and the notice for Vulgara the Insatiable made the rounds. Out in the highlands, Aelindra called for a group. Merith Gustwing and Evayne Greystorm were there when Vulgara fell. Aelindra thanked them and took the head back to claim the bounty. She had spent the morning learning what a hunter could do alone. It was pleasant to be reminded how quickly three people could get something done.

The missing windstones led to the Bandit Hideout. Aelindra gathered them back one at a time and went after the bandits' leader, a man called Badwind Bennic. He tried to break away more than once before she brought him down. By then she had a better idea of how much a stone was worth to a village. She wondered what it was worth to someone whose village had already fallen.

She reached her eighth level in the highlands. It was barely time to enjoy the thought before she fell at the Windsong Standing Stones. The details came back in pieces. There was the stone underfoot, then cold earth and the taste of dirt. She got up, brushed herself off, and headed back the way she had come. There was unfinished business. She brought in the Prideclaw pelts, returned the pilfered stones, and gave Aonda her report on Bennic. When she found a trainer again, she paid particular attention to the shot that could slow something down before it reached her.

Among the Faithful

The bandits had cost Shen'dar supplies. Aonda wanted to know what the Al'Aketh might cost it next. She sent Aelindra to Sania Silverstream, who warned her what happened to people who approached the Windlord's faithful under false pretenses. It was the sort of warning that stayed with a person even after the speaker had finished.

Aelindra passed Windsong Lake on her way to Falaath Village. Old constructs lay beneath those waters, relics of the days when the spirits still gave gifts and the pylons held steady. She kept walking. At Falaath, she reported to Missionary Jasaan and looked around among the faithful until she had enough to take back to Aonda. The constable had further work in mind, in the highlands and at the Western Watch, with stolen supplies still to recover. Aelindra took the assignments. She could leave those roads for another day.

She still owed Zerril something for the larder. Eggs and strider meat were ordinary things to carry back to a village that spent so much time talking about the fate of the sky. He had a use for both, and she was glad to set them down. She returned to her skinning trainer for the next lesson, then finally let herself stop at the Calmbreeze Inn.

Coriella Calmbreeze had time to talk. Aelindra was grateful for a conversation that could take as long as it took, with a chair under her and her bow close at hand. She found a little grave dirt still caught in the seam of one boot and scraped it loose with her thumbnail. When she looked up again, she caught herself listening for the wind outside.

At dawn she had listened for the sound of a pylon. Here, she listened until Coriella spoke again.

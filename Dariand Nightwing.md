# Dariand Nightwing

Night elf rogue. Born before the Great Sundering. Skin the color of old bark. White hair pulled into a long tail. Face weathered past the point of looking surprised by anything. He stabs backs for a living now. He did not always.

## Before the Sundering

Dariand was born in the forests around Mount Hyjal in the years before the War of the Ancients. The world was still one continent. The Well of Eternity still stood. He was young by the standards of that age when the Highborne began their work that would tear the world apart. He does not speak of the Sundering itself. When pressed he says the ground moved and the sky changed and the people he had known were on the other side of water that had not been there the day before. He walked north with the survivors who reached Hyjal. The Long Vigil began. He stayed.

## The Long Vigil

For the thousands of years that followed he was a tracker and boundary-walker in the Ashenvale and Hyjal forests. He read trails, set snares, kept the edges free of the things that did not belong, and reported what the Sentinels did not have time to see. The work was quiet. It paid in meat, in quiet nights, and in the knowledge that the people behind him were still there when he came back. He grew old by the only measure that mattered then: experience. Immortality meant the years did not show on the face the way they would later.

When Nordrassil burned at the end of the Third War the immortality ended. The future became finite. Some of the younger ones spoke of the new tree Fandral Staghelm was growing off the northern coast. Dariand listened. The old forests were changing. The corruption that had always lived in the deeper places was moving closer to the edges he walked. He did not argue when the call came to move.

## Darnassus

He went to Teldrassil when the city was still new. Darnassus was being built among the branches. The tree was young. The streets were still finding their shape. He took work as a tracker on the outer reaches of the new wood, the same work he had always done. For a short time the edges held. Then the corruption that Fandral’s choices had invited into the tree began to show in the places the Sentinels preferred not to look too closely. Trails that had been clean grew strange. Things that should not have been able to cross the boundary did. The people he reported to asked for clearer answers than the forest was willing to give.

He stayed longer than some. He walked the new edges the way he had walked the old ones. When the corruption grew thick enough that the work no longer felt like protection and started to feel like waiting for the next thing to come through, he stopped. The settlement he had known before was already gone. The new city was not going to keep the forest clean by wishing. He packed what he carried and left the tree.

## The Turn

He is old for an elf who decides, late, that the clean work is finished. Most who take the rogue’s path start younger. Dariand’s hands are steady. The conscience was never the problem. What changed was the understanding that some doors do not open from the front, and some threats do not stop because they were asked. The loss of immortality made the math clearer. A life that ends is a life that can be spent. He chose to spend what remained on work that did not require him to stand on a boundary that the forest itself was no longer willing to keep.

He learned the knife the way he once learned a trail: by walking it until the shape of it lived in the feet. He is not elegant. He is not fast the way the younger ones are fast. He is patient, and he does not miss when the opening is there. The back is simply the side that does not see the strike coming. He has made his peace with that math.

## Present

Dariand Nightwing works where the contract is clear and the target is already decided by someone else. He does not choose the names. He does not ask why the name was chosen. He takes the coin, does the work, and leaves before the questions start. The white tail and the weathered face make him easy to remember and hard to place. Most who meet him assume he is older than he looks and quieter than he is.

He does not live in Darnassus now. The city is still new. The tree is still young. The corruption that drove him out has not finished with either. He walks the edges when the work is finished, the way he always did. The difference is that the edges now belong to whoever is paying for the silence behind them. The years ahead are no longer endless. He treats them accordingly.

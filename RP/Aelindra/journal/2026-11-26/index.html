The Gryphons and the Trolls of Jintha'Alor

Farewell to the Desert

Sylthara and I began our morning beneath the wide, cloudless skies of Tanaris. Before leaving the desert, I settled an outstanding obligation to Security Chief Bilgewhizzle, recovering a bracelet lost among the Dunemaul ogres.

The goblins of the Steamwheedle Cartel have made a living in a land where even finding water is an accomplishment. I have come to respect their ingenuity, though I was ready to exchange the relentless sands for green hills and cooler air.

Our road eventually brought us to the Hinterlands, where forested mountains rise above the country of the Wildhammer dwarves. There, for the first time in some while, the lands below reminded me of the home above.

Among the Wildhammer

Aerie Peak is unlike the other dwarven settlements I have encountered. Rather than conceal their halls deep within the earth, the Wildhammers have built their home against the open mountainside, beneath a great stone likeness of a gryphon.

Their ancestors abandoned Grim Batol after the War of the Three Hammers. They made a new home beneath the sky, and their friendship with the gryphons became so strong that their riders once helped turn back the invading Horde during the Second War.

I found something familiar in that devotion. We Skyborne understand that the heavens are more than empty space above our heads. To live beneath an open sky is to know a particular kind of freedom.

I helped Ambassador Tove Redstone recover a missing diplomatic satchel before attending to the troubles of the Wildhammers. Sylthara and I investigated the Witherbark cages, collected troll necklaces and recovered six gryphon eggs.

Of all the tasks entrusted to me, the rescue of those eggs mattered most. The necklaces had a price, but what value could one place upon a creature that had yet to spread its wings?

It was at Aerie Peak that I reached my forty-sixth season. I returned briefly to Stormwind for instruction, then made my way back to the Hinterlands. I also prepared more bandages, for the mountains would soon give me ample reason to appreciate that particular craft.

The Heights of Jintha'Alor

The Vilebranch trolls hold a formidable city upon the mountainside. Jintha'Alor was raised by the ancient Amani, long before the present struggles between dwarves and forest trolls. Its great stone terraces ascend toward a place of sacrifice, and the Vilebranch guard their stronghold fiercely.

I joined three fellow adventurers: Korgrum, a dwarven paladin; Eoghli, a dwarven rogue; and Valeo, a human paladin. Having companions beside me was a welcome comfort, though even together we found the heights unforgiving.

Sylthara and I fought among the terraces, where every ascent seemed to bring us into the reach of another defender. A Vilebranch Hideskinner brought me down, and later a Shadow Hunter's arrow proved equally unforgiving. There was little dignity in returning to the fight after such defeats, but there was purpose in doing so.

I have spent much of my life learning to judge distances from above. Here, among narrow approaches and stone walls, I was reminded that the ground has its own rules. Courage does not make an arrow miss, nor does determination make a careless step any wiser.

Nevertheless, we persevered. I secured the Mallet of Zul'Farrak, a prize that would serve me when I eventually returned to the desert and ventured into that ancient troll city.

I also came away with a curious mechanical distress beacon. The ground-dwellers possess some remarkable inventions. I wonder what manner of creature, or contraption, might be calling for assistance.

Preparations for the Road Ahead

Back in Stormwind, a priest named Claire Weston helped improve my gloves with an enchantment. Bram Copperkeep also sent three pieces of equipment by post, and I later acquired an Ebon Shiv and another weapon while attending to my affairs in Ironforge.

Gold disappears remarkably quickly when one is preparing for the next adventure. Still, a hunter's equipment is more than an ornament. Every improvement may mean another arrow loosed before an enemy reaches us, another opportunity to protect Sylthara, or another evening safely spent beside a hearth.

A Thanksgiving in Goldshire

Shortly after midday, I returned to the Lion's Pride Inn in Goldshire. The familiar village seemed especially welcoming after the day's struggles.

There was much for which to be thankful. Sylthara, my faithful hunting companion, had shared another day's dangers with me. Little Sammie, the Siamese cat I had taken into my care, was a quieter reminder that companionship need not always be earned upon a battlefield.

I thought of the Wildhammers and their gryphons, of the eggs rescued from the trolls, and of the companions who had stood beside me on those ancient terraces. The world beneath the clouds is filled with dangers, but it is also filled with people and creatures worth knowing and protecting.

I have traveled far from the islands of my birth. Yet increasingly, I find that home is not necessarily the place where one's journey began.

Sometimes, it is the place where one feels welcome upon returning.

— Aelindra Ser'jaen'thal

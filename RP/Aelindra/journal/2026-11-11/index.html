11 November 2026

The Lights of Lordaeron

I was in Raven Hill before the sun came up. In Duskwood there is remarkably little difference between the hour before sunrise and the hour after it. The houses stood empty under the trees, and I came away from my errand with a Night Watchman’s Torch. I was glad of it. On Zephras, if I wanted more light, I could usually find more sky.

Westfall had wind enough to clear the cobwebs out of my head. I spent the better part of an hour following Captain Sander’s clues, from the Longshore past Jangolode Mine and back toward the coast. The captain had left a shirt, a sash, some silver, and a locker. He had also left me with the habit of looking twice at anything that seemed to have been abandoned. At Alexston Farmstead, among the burned remains, I found another note. That one went into my pack for later.

By the time I’d been through Menethil Harbor on an errand involving Tom’s gift, I’d found something I’d wanted since yesterday. There were people going to the Ruins of Lordaeron, and they had room for me.

The Ruins of Lordaeron

Getting five people to the same gate was an adventure of its own. There was considerable debate over Silverpine, the road, and whether the lake was worth the trouble. I came through Hillsbrad, past the farms and up by the Misty Shore. I remember the relief of being able to tell the others, “i can see the city.” Then I had to ask which door to use. Mia was already inside. Main gate, left-hand side, she told me.

People still speak of the day Arthas came home and killed his father in the throne room. I had heard the name Lordaeron before. Standing inside the walls, I found myself looking at the doorways and wondering how many people had stood there waiting for their prince to come home. Then Sylthara caught sight of something low to the ground, and I had a more immediate use for my eyes.

We started among the spiders while people were still finding their way in. By the time we faced Witherfang, it was Egg Eggon in front, Mia Lichlore healing, Elkon Norres working his spells, and Aldrin Stockholm and me shooting. Egg consecrated the ground beneath the spider. I marked it and fired while Sylthara got close enough to rake it. Witherfang’s poison was the danger. Mia kept her healing on the company while we kept our attention on the spider, and it fell before we’d had much time to get used to fighting together.

The skeletons gave us plenty of practice. Bloodied insignia turned up among the bones, and we stopped to gather them as we went. At Lordamere Overlook there were skeletons ahead of us, Sylthara at my side, and the green light of Elkon’s magic cutting across the dark. Somewhere in that press I reached my nineteenth level of training. There was time to be pleased about it afterward.

In King’s Alley, the Baron called out for fresh meat for the block. I understood the name Butcher as soon as he came at us. He hit hard enough to knock a fighter senseless and trampled through the space Egg was trying to hold. Egg answered with the Hammer of Justice. Mia was casting one heal after another, and Aldrin and I kept firing while Sylthara clawed at the Baron from close in. His last words were, “Forgive me, Lordaeron...” I’d been glad to hear him fall. I was less glad to hear him speak.

Elkon offered us healthstones before we met the Abandoned. I could see why once the cold came. Frost caught at our feet, and the creature drew life out of the living. Egg called on a blessing of freedom in the middle of it. I could still shoot from where I stood, so I kept the mark on the creature and fired while Sylthara went in again. We barely had time to get warm before Bjork gave Egg another hard fight. Bjork put up a shield against magic and struck with enough force to make me grateful for the space between us. I had ordinary shot to spare. Sylthara had claws to spare. We both used them.

We found Rath’mael in the graveyard. He called us unfortunate souls, which struck me as a peculiar greeting from someone about to set the ground alight. Flamestrike came down among us. Egg kept after him, Mia kept the company on its feet, and Aldrin and I fired while Sylthara closed in. “Even in death... I will persist,” he said as he fell. There was a blood-stained letter in that graveyard. I took it with me. After a morning of prying insignia out of the ruins, it felt different to carry away something one person had meant for another.

There was still Viktor the Vile. We had scarcely finished looking around before Elkon said, “oh i did a thing.” Aldrin’s answer came quick. “now youve done it.” Viktor gave us a last round of leeching poison. Egg held the front, Mia went back to healing, and I marked Viktor and kept the gun going while Sylthara tore at him. When it was over, Elkon observed, “theres a lesson here about lighting fires in ruins.” I expect there is. I was laughing too much to ask him to state it plainly.

We stayed to find the Crest of Lordaeron and went back to Captain Truman. Egg made a point of thanking Mia, which I was glad to hear. I thanked all of them. Yesterday I’d spent a good part of the day trying to find a company for these ruins. Today I’d have been happy to go somewhere else with this same one.

A Letter for Bram

In Stormwind I turned in the crest and the bloodied insignia. The letter went to Orphan Matron Nightingale. I came away with a tarnished locket and found myself thinking about it again later, when I sat down to work leather. It was good to have something plain to do with my hands. I made armor kits, stitched gloves and cloaks, and did enough work to earn my journeyman training.

At the inn in Lakeshire, I wrote to Bram Copperkeep and sent him an enchanted belt. I’ve hired him as my Adventurer’s Factor. He can see to business in Ironforge while I’m out on the road, and I can stop carrying perfectly saleable things from one end of the world to the other. I’d also been doing errands around Lake Everstill. One of the men there remarked on how far we were from the sea for there to be murlocs in the lake. I’d been thinking about how to get rid of them. Now I wonder what might have driven them inland.

The note from Alexston Farmstead was still in my pack. I went back to the Westfall coast and swam south to Booty Bay. I’d heard enough about the roads through Stranglethorn to keep the shore on my left and keep swimming. From Booty Bay I took the boat to Ratchet, then followed the note to a burned tower near the Field of Giants. There I picked up the next clue, Stepping Stones.

I made it as far as Desolace before I stopped for the night. I shook out my old bedroll and set up camp. Sylthara circled the spot once before settling beside me. Tomorrow we’ll follow the next clue. Tonight, the old bedroll will do, if she leaves me enough of it.

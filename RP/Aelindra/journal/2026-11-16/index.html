The Road Again

By the time Fawney Catspirit invited me to Blackfathom Deeps, I had already crossed the sea twice and was on the far side of it again. I’d shown a prehistoric prism to High Explorer Magellas in Ironforge, carried word of a strange bloom through Menethil and Auberdine, and gone out to the Lost Fleet to lift a curse. I brought the Eye of Paleth back to Glorin Steelbrow with the smell of the wrecks still on me. Then came Fiora Longears in Theramore and Shindrell Swiftfire in Astranaar. There’s a point in a morning like that when you stop putting the map away.

I took on the Aspect of the Cheetah wherever the ground opened up. Sylthara could go from a run to a crouch so smoothly I’d almost miss it. I was still learning when to do the same. The bends around Greenpaw Village, the Shrine of Aessina, and Lake Falathim gave me plenty of practice before the trees thinned onto the Zoram Strand. I’d told Fawney I was running their way. I was beginning to wonder whether that counted as news.

Beneath the strand

Fawney had been gathering people while I ran. Static Hazards wanted time to pick up the errands for the Deeps, so Fawney shared what they could. Rioren Windstep was on the way. I asked if we could bring in Timmay Birch, a druid, and after a little trouble getting the invitation through, there were five of us. Timmay knew the entrance. I managed to get all the way to the ruins and still had to ask where it was. Fawney suggested waiting by the fountain so nobody got left behind among the guards.

The sea had swallowed an old temple of Elune here. Water still divided what had once been rooms and walkways. I sent Sylthara ahead where there was stone enough for her to get her footing, then found enough room to bring my gun up. Ghamoo-ra came first, then Geilhast among the murlocs. After Geilhast, Fawney called us over to find Thaelrid. I was glad someone was looking for him while the rest of us were looking for the next thing coming at us.

We brought down Lady Sarevess and promptly had to work out where to go. Fawney wondered whether we’d turned back toward the entrance. Static thought the passage led to the last chamber. Timmay checked a map and pointed us upward. There are easier places to discover that everyone remembers a different doorway.

We stopped for a breather before going deeper. Fawney came back to find what they called a ghost crab attacking them. By then I was ready to believe any crab in the place had unfinished business. Timmay drew out the defenders in the next room. We put down Twilight Lord Kelris, then came the candles and, at last, Aku’mai. Timmay warned us the beast might send us flying. It went down without making good on the warning.

Rioren spotted that there was a way straight to Darnassus. I was one corrupted brain stem short, and Timmay offered to walk out with me and help find it. I had the last one before we reached daylight. Outside, Timmay laid out a bedroll by the fire, and Fawney offered a banner. We’d spent so long picking places to stand that it felt good to sit where someone else had already thought to make room.

“May the winds favor you Timmay,” I told the druid before we parted. I meant it for the whole company.

From salt water to snow

I put the Cheetah back on and crossed Ashenvale again, past Splintertree Post and Satyrnaar to Kayneth Stillwind at Forest Song. I reached my thirtieth level that morning. There were still Blackfathom errands to settle and the Eye to take to Archbishop Benedictus in Stormwind. Each time I handed something over, somebody seemed to have another name for me and a rough idea of how far away it was.

On the Hillsbrad coast, Issia Windsinger invited me to hunt the Torn Fin with her. We worked the same stretch of shore, gathering murloc heads for her proof and counting oracles for my own errand. There was a chest along the way. Issia quietly told me that when a party finds one in the wild, the polite thing is to roll for it. She’d only meant to show me where it was. I’ll remember the courtesy. We stayed until she had her tenth head and I had my tenth oracle. Then she asked if I needed anything else before we went our separate ways.

From Southshore I went up into the foothills past the broken tower at Gallows’ Corner to Sofera’s Naze, where the Syndicate had made camp. There were plans and an encrypted letter to account for, and mountain lions enough to make me choose carefully when to run and when to send Sylthara ahead. The day took me as far as Ironforge’s Tinker Town and snowy Kharanos on gnomish business. Then I was back among the foothills and on toward Lordamere. At the internment camp above Dalaran I finally answered the alarming request I’d been carrying since early afternoon. I came away with a sewer key and more work to do in Dalaran’s shadow. I put the key deep in my pouch. I’d had enough of wet stairs for one day.

Somehow there was still time to reach Booty Bay and hand in word at the Salty Sailor before turning north again. In Ironforge, Prospector Stormpike read the letter I brought him. He could make out the words and still couldn’t tell what the thieves meant by them. He wanted Magistrate Maleb in Southshore to see it.

That was where I reached thirty-two, in a room full of books with another letter to carry. Of course it was Southshore again. I can picture the road down to the water now without opening the map. I think Sylthara could find it without me.

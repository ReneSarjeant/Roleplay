# Roric Stonehearth

Ironforge dwarf. Innkeeper turned Knight of the Silver Hand. 187 in year 25. Spent most of his years behind a bar in the Commons. His nephew Brenn did not come back from a caravan run. The door he kept was not wide enough.

## The Common Room

Roric Stonehearth spent most of his long life behind a bar.

He was not a warrior by trade. He was an innkeeper in the Commons of Ironforge—the kind of place where travelers off the roads, miners coming up from the deeps, and dwarves who just needed a warm corner and a full tankard could sit without being asked too many questions. He poured the ale, kept the fire going, and broke up the fights that stayed at the level of fists and hard words. Every dwarf in Khaz Modan grows up knowing how to swing something heavy if the door needs holding, but Roric had always preferred keeping the peace inside his own walls. The work suited him. People came and went. The regulars knew his name. The door stayed open.

His nephew Brenn was different. Younger, quicker, full of the restless energy Roric had mostly set aside. Brenn took work with caravans running supplies between Ironforge and the human kingdoms. He talked about the roads, the open country, the chance to see something beyond the mountain. Roric grumbled the way uncles do—said the work was dangerous, said a dwarf with sense stayed where the ale was cold and the doors were thick—and still slipped him an extra coin and a good knife before every run. He told himself it was just practical. Brenn never argued about it.

## The Caravan

The caravan never came back from one of those runs. Word reached the Commons that orcs had hit it hard in the foothills. They had not stopped at the supplies. Brenn did not make it. The news arrived on a quiet afternoon while Roric was wiping down the bar. He finished the shift. He closed up. He sat with a tankard he did not drink until the fire burned low and the common room was empty.

He did not go looking for a fight the next morning. He went to the Hall of Mysteries.

## The Hall

He had heard the Light’s teachings for years—human allies, dwarven priests, the same three virtues repeated in the halls—but he had always figured it was for other people. The ones who felt called to banners and plate. Brenn’s death made the old idea of “looking after my own” feel too small. If the roads outside his door were not safe, then the people who walked through his door were not really safe either. The trainers did not turn him away. They had taken stout-hearted dwarves after the wars before. Roric was older than most of the squires. He was slower with the formal doctrine and quicker with a practical question than a pious one. He trained anyway.

He learned to wear the plate. He learned to call the Light when his hands were not steady. He learned that justice was measured by more than the size of the hammer. The weight of it was different from a tankard or a bar rag. Some days it still sits strange on shoulders that spent decades behind a counter.

After he took up the plate he learned Mining and Blacksmithing. The armor needed the ore and the forge. He had not needed either while he was only keeping the common room. Fishing was already his. He still prefers the line when the work is done.

## The Light

He still keeps the inn. Some nights he pours for the regulars and listens the way he always has. Other nights the plate is packed and he walks the roads or answers when the Alliance calls. Brenn’s knife is still on the shelf behind the bar. Roric has not moved it. He does not talk about the caravan much. When the day is done he wipes the counter, checks the lock, and makes sure the door he used to keep is not the only one that matters anymore.

The Light, as he understands it, has room for an old innkeeper who decided the common room was not wide enough. He does not preach a quota. He shows up. Grief still sits close, but the work is the same as it ever was: keep the people under your roof safe. He just had to learn that the roof was larger than he thought.

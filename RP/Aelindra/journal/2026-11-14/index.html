The Hall Behind Us

From Aelindra Sarjeant’s journal, 14 November, Year 25

At the Falfarren River, I learned a better way to use a claw. Bringing that lesson back to Sylthara took less explaining than most things do in Common. She already knew how to bite hard enough to make a creature wish it had chosen another patch of ground. Now she could strike harder with her forepaws, too. I was pleased with us both. By noon I would have cause to wish I’d spent as much time learning what she hears behind her.

The Wetlands were grey clear to the hilltops. At Whelgar’s dig the dwarves were still pulling fragments of their past out of the earth, and up at Dun Modr the Dark Iron had left them more recent troubles. I finished what work I could and delivered a crimson crate before riding on. Later, in Duskwood, I took word back to Jitters and followed the first scraps of an old story about a man named Stalvan. I left it there for the morning. I had another man’s name to follow, one I’d brought away from the Deadmines.

Bazil Thredd had once worked beside the men who rebuilt Stormwind. Above his prison were their white walls, their bridges, and people going about their errands. Below, Warden Thelwater had a riot on his hands. And somewhere in the city was a grandmother named Nikova who wanted ten red bandanas because her grandson Mac had been killed wearing a guard’s badge at fourteen. I tried to hold all three things in my head as we went down the stairs.

The first pull

There were five of us: Aveline Clarke, Gloria Renn, Durak Stonehelm, Silent, and me, with Sylthara at my heel. I’d spent some time looking for a healer. Once Gloria was there, we went around the company for a word of readiness. Everybody gave one.

The first rush carried farther than we expected. Defias came out of the cells and Targorr the Dread came into the fight before we’d settled the first of them. Gloria got a healing spell through to me. I loosed an arrow while I still had room to draw. Then a captive and an inmate closed in from either side. I took up my lance and held it short between us. The captive struck again, and the stone took my weight.

They fought on after I fell. Targorr went down less than half a minute later, while I still lay under the torch with my lance on the floor. Then Aveline fell, too. It took us longer to come back together than it had taken the prison to scatter us. When we went in again I collected Targorr’s head and got Sylthara back into the fight. I was glad to hear her claws on the stone.

The passage behind me

We made headway after that. By the time we reached Dextren Ward, we’d begun to find a rhythm. When Ward fell I took his hand for the man who’d asked for proof. A few minutes later I backed into the passage to give myself a clear shot with the bow.

I could see the fight ahead of me clearly. That was what made the spot seem so good.

An insurgent came at me in the hall. Then a convict was close enough to hit me, and an inmate joined them. I had a room full of companions a few strides away, with more Defias between us than there had been when I stepped back. Aveline and Gloria called on the Light almost on the same breath. I saw Aveline get her feet under her again. A few seconds later she went down anyway.

Afterward I told them I’d been too far into the hall and a patrol had caught me. Aveline said she needed to mend her gear. Gloria had already apologized, and Aveline had already told her it was all right. We waited for her to come back. I’d been left behind at Shadowfang the day before. This time there was somebody to wait for, and I was glad we did.

Kam Deepfury went down quickly once we got to him. Hamhock started to call lightning in a room where it would have had a short road from one of us to the next. Silent kicked him before he could finish. Then came Thredd. The blows that stunned Aveline kept coming until she fell again. Gloria took two herself. In the little openings we had, Sylthara and I kept at it, close and far, until Thredd went down. We gathered the last of the red cloth and took the proof Thelwater wanted.

We tried to hurry out. That was another lesson. I fell again, within a much shorter run of daylight than I’d have liked, and Durak fell a few breaths after me. By the time we parted I had enough breath to tell them what I meant. “You have my gratitude.”

Above the canals

Thelwater had more to tell once he saw what we’d brought him. Thredd had been receiving visits from a man who called himself Maelik. Baros Alexston sent me to Mathias Shaw, who knew the visitor by another name, Marzon. That trail took me to Elling Trias and left me with work still to do at the keep. I’d gone below the city to find out what a prisoner knew. I came back with a name the city had let through the door.

I delivered the red bandanas, and in the afternoon I picked up Stalvan’s trail again. It went through old records and letters, by way of Goldshire and back into Duskwood. Manor Mistmantle had gone to rot. There was one flower still growing in the garden, and when Stalvan called out for Tilloa it was Sylthara he saw. The first time I faced him, I fell. I came back with company and brought the Mistmantle ring to Darkshire. Later, at the hunter’s trainer in the Dwarven District, I worked on my shot, learned a frost trap, and took another lesson in tending Sylthara’s wounds.

There’s room in Stormwind to stand back and look up. I took a moment for that after the lesson. Then I looked down at Sylthara. One of her ears had turned toward the street behind us. I turned to see what she’d heard.

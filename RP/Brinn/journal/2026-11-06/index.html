Sister Brinn Sarjeant · 6 November · Year 25 ADP

Westfall

When Brinn came downstairs at the Lion’s Pride, yesterday’s rain was gone. The floor by the door had dried in patches. She tightened a strap on one of Du Track’s linen bags and set out before there was much light to see by.

At the Tower of Azora, Hamish had his business with the rogue wizards. Brinn had a question of her own. Who were these Defias bandits, and why were they everywhere she went? By the time she crossed into Westfall, she had seen enough of them to know what they could do. She still couldn’t have said what they wanted.

The Furlbrows were by the road. Brinn handed the farmer his deed, taking care with the fold where the paper had begun to split. Across the way, a harvest golem worked its way through the field. Its arms swung with the steady rhythm of somebody who had done the same job all his life.

The farmer had the paper back. His farm was another matter.

A high five at Forest’s Edge

At Westbrook Garrison, Deputy Rainer had a wanted notice for Hogger. Brinn had seen the Riverpaw armbands and heard what the gnolls did to farms along the road. She went into the woods expecting trouble. Hogger gave her more than she had allowed for.

Joe Dynamite came in while she was still trying to get clear. Brinn would later write that the gnome had saved her from certain death. What she remembered afterward was how quickly it was over, and how readily Joe raised his palm for a high five. Hers was still shaking a little when it met his.

She stayed to collect the armbands. In another fight at Forest’s Edge she used Mind Blast for the first time. She had known the words since yesterday. Saying them with something coming at her was easier than she’d expected, and that gave her something to think about when there was time.

“Are you ok?” called Lysander Little a little later. When she didn’t answer at once, he tried her name.

“Yes, thank you.”

He’d seen the movements she made while fighting and thought something was wrong.

“No, it is the wand,” Brinn told him.

Lysander said he generally smashed things with his mace and cast with the spirits. This seemed to account for the misunderstanding to both their satisfaction. Brinn wished him well and went back to collecting armbands.

Under a mountain, above the road

An errand for Grimand Elmore carried her through Stormwind and onto the Deeprun Tram. She held the rail through the tunnel and let go when the carriage stopped. A few minutes later, at the Great Forge, she stopped dead of her own accord.

She’d known there would be a forge. She hadn’t expected a city built around the heat of it. Molten rock ran down the cavern walls, and people went about their errands with the light of it on their faces. When she came out at the gates, she looked back at the enormous statue of High King Modimus Anvilmar. Beyond it lay snow. It took her eyes a moment to get used to the white.

At the North Gate Outpost, she helped Pilot Hammerfoot look for a missing pilot and dealt with Mangeclaw. She delivered Elmore’s package at Algaz Station, made it as far as Thelsamar, and used her hearthstone to get back to Goldshire. By breakfast she had asked the cook what else he could teach her. There was a great deal to be said for a morning that ended with somebody putting a plate down in front of you.

Later, at Sentinel Hill, Quartermaster Lewis sent her to Stormwind with a note. Thor put her on a gryphon. The first sweep of its wings took the ground out from under her so fast she forgot to be afraid until there was nothing to do about it. Then the roads shrank to threads, the treetops streamed beneath her, and she wanted to see where every one of those roads went.

What people carry

Back in Westfall, Verna Furlbrow needed oats for Old Blanchy. Her husband wanted a pocket watch from the wardrobe at their old farm. Brinn brought both. The watch was a small thing to have left behind and a hard thing to go back for. She was glad to see it in the farmer’s hands.

At the Saldeans’, there was stew to make and company on the road. Someone called, “Light bless you all!” Brinn wished them a good day and went in to Salma. The harvest golems were still out in the rows. People could still get a meal at this house. Salma was doing what she could about the part that lay within reach.

Brinn had spent part of the afternoon finding out just how badly a mine could go when she pressed on alone. In Stormwind, when Allustriel Rainshadow asked for a hand with a containment job, Brinn had one piece of advice.

“Do it upstairs.”

Allustriel listened. A little later she had filled her coffers and was thanking Brinn. It was a relief to have something to show for a bad experience besides the memory of it.

There was more riding between Westfall, Stormwind, and Lakeshire. In Lakeshire she found Hilary’s necklace, brought a guard his lunch, and took daffodils to Darcy. There were reports to carry, too. Wiley’s note passed from Gryan Stoutmantle to Mathias Shaw and back again as a report. At SI:7, Brinn heard a suggestion that the Brotherhood’s troubles went further back than she’d thought, perhaps as far as some quarrel in Stormwind. She left with the papers and more questions than she’d brought.

Room for sixteen

By evening she had been back through Dun Morogh, where the snow had turned heavy, and was in Westfall again. At Sentinel Hill, Chriandre Wolfheart was gathering guildmates to follow a trail of notes. At the end of it, they were told, there was a sleeping bag.

The first clue took them to Alexston Farmstead. After that came boats, the gray coast at Auberdine, and the old trees of Ashenvale, thick enough to keep much of the rain above their heads. The Barrens opened out all at once. They passed through Ratchet and found the charred clue in the Field of Giants. At Mirkfallon Lake, in Stonetalon, someone had left the next note fixed to a plank.

Brinn had thought the gryphon would be the day’s great journey. Now she was crossing the Stonewrought Dam in the dark, with tired people on either side of her and still another scrap of a clue to follow. The Wetlands cost them time. There was more trouble near Refuge Pointe. People stopped for those who’d fallen and called out where to find them. Then they got moving again.

They reached Thoradin’s Wall late. Brinn counted sixteen who had finished. Someone fell asleep almost at once. Gragnar Cragshaper looked at his new bedding and asked, “why is my sleeping bag soooo big”.

By then Brinn could think of several excellent reasons to have a sleeping bag that big. The first was that she could get both feet in it. The second was that, after the day she’d had, she meant to stay there a while.

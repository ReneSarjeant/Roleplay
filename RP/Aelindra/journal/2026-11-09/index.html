Where the Wind Carried Us

I left the Valanaar inn with Sylthara at my heel and meant to be back before the morning was out. There were still people waiting for news from Ban’aethal. In the ruins we gathered what the refugees had been forced to leave behind. I found Resaan Nimbuswalker’s heirloom there, too, and brought it back to Ealaane. A thing small enough to close a hand around can be a heavy thing to carry. I was glad to put it into hers.

The fighting took us through the Shrine of Akir and up to the Rohashi Spires. Elaadrin had hoped for more reinforcements. We went with what we had. Ayessa Dawnsinger stood with him when we found High Priestess Lorthuna, and it was Ayessa who asked her to stop. Lorthuna had once been her sister among the Windshapers. I wondered how long it had been since either of them had spoken that word kindly.

We thought we had come to keep her from bringing down the wards. Lorthuna told us they had been down for days. All she needed now was a light bright enough for the servants of Al’Akir to find us.

Rohash answered.

The Lord of the East Wind sounded almost puzzled at first. He looked about the Spires as if he had found a room in his own house that someone had kept locked against him. Lorthuna demanded to be taken before the Windlord so he could learn who had opened the way. Rohash cast her aside. Then he told us what a pleasure it would be to tell his lord where we were.

Duke Anvillaxx fell in the fighting that followed. I remember looking down afterward and finding my fingers still curled as if there were another arrow between them. Then Archmage Ansirem Runeweaver came through from Azeroth with Muln Earthfury. Ansirem had persuaded Rhonin to help open a passage between the planes. Muln asked Rohash to leave us in peace. The Lord of the East Wind agreed to go, and promised we would see him again.

Before we returned to Valanaar, Ayessa welcomed Muln on behalf of the Windshapers, and Elaadrin welcomed Ansirem and his Kirin Tor. I had heard plenty about what separated our two orders. I thought I would remember the sight of their leaders standing together at the Spires for rather longer.

I boarded the skycutter at the Valanaar Skydocks. Zephras slipped astern while Sylthara found a place to crouch against my boots. The deck moved under me in a way I knew. I rested my fingers on my mother’s pearls and watched as long as there was anything left of home to see. At Dalaran I reported to Denaaris Stargale, then took the one-way portal to Stormwind. I came out on stone with no give in it at all.

Bolvar Fordragon sent me to meet the people of the Alliance. By the time I was done I had called on Magni Bronzebeard, High Tinker Mekkatorque, and Tyrande Whisperwind. I had also rounded up rats in the Deeprun Tram and carried word to a man named Nipsy. I suspect the Alliance would have introduced itself differently if it had known how much I would learn from the rats. There is a knack to making yourself useful in a place where nobody knows your name yet.

The rest of the day went much the same way. I carried a delivery for the Stormpikes and followed the trail of a missing pilot in Dun Morogh. There was business in Auberdine, word to carry for the marshal at Sentinel Hill, and more miles between Westfall and Stormwind than I cared to count. At Lakeshire, I found Hilary’s necklace in the lake. She told me she could speak kitty and that Effsee had said thank you. Sylthara, sitting beside me on the dock, had the good sense to let the other cat speak for herself.

I finished the day in Ironforge. Earthseer Farsen had sent me to take the head of Durgen Dirgehammer. In the city, Afadra Dunwall told me about the restless dead, and Thom Filch asked me to look for the heirlooms left below. I thought of Ealaane closing her hand around what I had brought her that morning. I made sure I had room in my pack.

The Hall of Thanes lies beneath Old Ironforge. I checked my bow and counted my arrows. Sylthara waited beside me. I have spent my whole life judging the space beneath my feet by the feel of the wind. Soon I mean to find out what it is like to have a mountain overhead.

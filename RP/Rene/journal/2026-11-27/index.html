A Pocketful of Bolts and a Handful of Fire

A Small Mage in a Large World

There is a particular disadvantage to being a gnome in Dun Morogh. Snow that reaches a dwarf's ankles comes considerably farther up one's trousers.

I shall have to do something about that.

My adventures began in Coldridge Valley, beneath mountains white with snow and among dwarves who seemed remarkably accustomed to the cold.

Sten Stoutarm needed wolf meat. Talin Keeneye had trouble with boars. Neither assignment sounded particularly complicated, although the wolves proved rather less cooperative than I had anticipated.

Magic is an interesting craft. Unlike a mechanical spring, which generally behaves according to its construction, a spell requires considerable attention to one's concentration.

And wolves are exceedingly distracting.

I also delivered letters between the dwarven camps and visited Marryk Nurribit in Anvilmar to further my magical instruction. There is something wonderfully satisfying about getting a spell precisely right. It reminds me of the sound two perfectly cut gears make when they first turn together.

The Trouble with Troggs

Balir Frosthammer had discovered a growing threat from the Rockjaw troggs.

Troggs.

Of all the creatures beneath these mountains, I would have preferred to encounter almost anything else.

I remember what their arrival meant for Gnomeregan. The warnings, the hurried evacuation, the things left behind.

But these dwarves had given my people shelter. I could hardly refuse to help protect theirs.

I dealt with the troggs, located Grund and Gozwin's camp, and subsequently ventured among the Frostmane trolls at Grelin Whitebeard's request.

Their leader, Grik'nir the Cold, had possession of a stolen journal. He no longer has it.

Grelin does.

A satisfactory adjustment to the situation.

A Refugee's Quandary

Of all the people I helped today, Felix Whindlebolt was perhaps the one I understood best.

He needed someone to recover belongings scattered among the dangers of Coldridge Valley: a box, a chest, and a bucket of bolts.

Someone unfamiliar with our people might wonder why a bucket of bolts was worth risking one's neck.

I did not.

A collection of carefully selected parts can represent months of work. Sometimes those little pieces are all that remain of something much larger.

I returned his belongings to him in Anvilmar.

For a moment, I thought about a little clockwork bird with one unfinished wing.

Then I found something else to occupy my hands.

A Very Important Mug

Nori Pridedrift entrusted me with an exceptionally urgent delivery: a hot mug of Mornbrew for Durnan Furcutter.

Five minutes before it grew cold.

At last, an assignment with clearly defined engineering tolerances!

I hurried through the snow and delivered the beverage while it was still hot. Naturally, the empty mug then needed returning.

Dwarves take their beverages seriously. I find this rather admirable, although I suspect they could benefit from a properly insulated drinking vessel.

Perhaps a double-walled copper mug with a sealed air chamber?

I shall have to draw something.

Beyond Coldridge Pass

Having delivered Grelin's observations to Mountaineer Thalos, I passed beyond the valley and made my way toward Kharanos.

The mountains opened before me. Snow-covered pines, winding roads and distant peaks made the world seem considerably larger than it had from my little corner of Anvilmar.

Eric Brighthammer introduced me to some of the practical necessities of adventuring, including the usefulness of a properly constructed campfire.

Fire for warmth is considerably more pleasant than fire for persuading a troll to leave one alone.

I also practiced skinning throughout my travels. It is not quite the same as cutting cloth or working with fine metal, but there is precision involved, and the materials may prove useful.

I have also become a member of Mid Life Aggro. Adventuring among people with considerably more experience than myself should provide ample opportunities to learn.

Provided they don't insist on treating me like a child.

The Grizzled Den

My afternoon took me to Steelgrill's Depot, where I delivered tools and accepted several more obligations.

One involved wendigos.

I have discovered two important facts about wendigos.

First, they are considerably larger when standing immediately in front of you.

Second, I am not yet as accomplished a mage as I occasionally imagine myself to be.

Twice I fell during my encounters around the Grizzled Den, once to a wendigo shaman and once to another wendigo.

Rather embarrassing.

Nevertheless, I returned and continued my work. A failed experiment is not necessarily a wasted experiment, provided one remembers what went wrong.

I still have several matters to attend to in these mountains, including Mountaineer Gretchen's missing colleague and a delivery of candles to Father Gavin.

Tomorrow, perhaps.

A Warm Hearth in Kharanos

By evening, I had reached my sixth season of adventuring and returned to the warmth of the Thunderbrew Distillery in Kharanos.

The dwarven inn smelled of woodsmoke, cooked meat and strong drink. Outside, the wind continued its tireless work upon the mountains.

I settled near the hearth and examined my belongings.

My clothes needed attention. My magical technique needed improvement. Several people were awaiting my assistance.

And my curiosity remained entirely undiminished.

For the first time in quite a while, the world beyond Gnomeregan seemed full of possibilities rather than simply being the place to which we had escaped.

I have not forgotten the old workshop.

Nor the bird.

But there is still much that these hands can build.

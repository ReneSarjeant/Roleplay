Bram Copperkeep · 5 November · Year 25 ADP

From Coldridge to Commerce

Dun Morogh, Ironforge, and Stormwind

The Cold Morning

The morning began cold. Not the ordinary sort of cold that encouraged a dwarf to draw his cloak tighter and consider a second breakfast. This was the cold of Dun Morogh, where the wind found every gap in a man's clothing and snow settled precisely where it was least welcome.

Bram Copperkeep stood in Coldridge Valley, surveyed the mountains, and adjusted the strap of his pack. He had always considered himself a sensible dwarf. He kept his accounts properly, knew the value of a copper, and believed that anyone borrowing a man's tools ought to return them in better condition than most borrowers managed.

And now, for reasons he was beginning to question, he was about to spend his morning fighting wolves.

"Should've stayed in Ironforge," he muttered.

Still, there were things to be learned beyond the city gates, and a dwarf intending to handle the business of adventurers ought to understand the trouble they encountered. Besides, Sten Stoutarm needed wolf meat.

Bram pulled his weapon free and set about earning his breakfast.

A Dwarf's Work Is Never Done

The wolves were troublesome, but the troggs were worse. Balir Frosthammer had concerns about the creatures gathering around Coldridge. Rockjaw troggs had become bold enough to threaten the surrounding area, and somebody needed to put a stop to them.

Bram listened with the thoughtful expression of a man calculating whether the payment justified the inconvenience. "Six of each, you say?" He considered the task. "That's twelve troggs." He paused. "Just making certain we're counting the same way."

The work proved harder than the arithmetic. Troggs were ugly, aggressive, and entirely unwilling to negotiate a reasonable settlement. Bram discovered that a weapon and a firm stance were rather more useful than a carefully worded complaint.

His warrior training began to show results. He learned to raise his voice in a proper battle shout, to charge into a fight, and, eventually, to use Rend against his opponents.

That last lesson took some effort. He found himself short of the money needed for training. Bram stood outside the training hall, checked his purse, and gave a low grunt of displeasure.

"Well, that's embarrassing."

For a dwarf who intended to make his living managing other people's finances, being unable to afford his own instruction was particularly irritating. He returned to the valley and continued working until he had gathered enough copper.

When he finally learned Rend, he felt considerably better about the morning. "That's more like it."

Letters, Boars, and a Proper Cup of Mornbrew

The morning's assignments carried Bram across Coldridge Valley. Sten Stoutarm entrusted him with letters for Talin Keeneye, who had his own concerns about the local wildlife. The boars were getting out of hand.

Bram was beginning to suspect that everything in Coldridge Valley was getting out of hand.

"Any creature about here that isn't trying to bite somebody?"

Talin's request was straightforward: twelve small crag boars. Bram preferred straightforward instructions. He could count twelve boars. He could also count the coins he expected to earn afterward.

Before long, he was carrying another letter, this one bound for Grelin Whitebeard. Deliveries, he decided, were respectable work. They required punctuality, accuracy, and a certain degree of discretion. These were qualities Bram appreciated.

Unfortunately, the valley also seemed determined to involve him in every dispute between its inhabitants and the surrounding wildlife. By the time he reached Anvilmar, he was ready for something warm.

Nori Pridedrift provided an opportunity. A mug of scalding mornbrew needed delivering to Durnan Furcutter, and it needed to arrive before the drink cooled. Now this was a task Bram could understand: an important delivery, a clear destination, a deadline.

He took the mug and hurried off. Durnan received it with unmistakable enthusiasm. "There's nothing like some scalding mornbrew on a chilly Dun Morogh day to get things started right!"

A moment later, the dwarf discovered just how hot his drink was. "Oooooo hot hot hot! If that won't put spring in your step, I don't know what will!"

Bram regarded him for a moment. "Well, it certainly seems to have done something."

Then came another errand. The empty mug needed returning. Bram looked down at it. He had carried the drink there. Now he was carrying the mug back. He began to understand why professional couriers charged for their services.

Company in the Snow

As the morning progressed, Bram found himself among a small number of other travelers. The valley was quieter than he expected. For a time, he had almost believed himself the only soul abroad. Then he spotted others near Grelin Whitebeard.

"Wow, other people here this morning," he remarked.

A fellow traveler, Gimbelit Despawn, offered a greeting. "Hello."

Bram nodded in acknowledgment. "It is so quiet this morning, I love it."

"Very nice," Gimbelit agreed.

Nearby, Metrion Zynthos had his own opinion about the weather. "Cold winter nights, my favorite."

Bram glanced toward the surrounding mountains. "Aye, though there's something to be said for a warm bed and a roof that doesn't leak."

He was pleased to see other folk on the road, even if he wasn't inclined to surrender the peaceful morning to a great deal of unnecessary noise. The quiet suited him. There was room to think, room to work, and room to hear one's own boots crunching through the snow. A dwarf could accomplish quite a lot under those conditions.

Trouble Among the Trolls

Grelin Whitebeard had more serious concerns. Frostmane trolls were causing trouble, and the situation had grown beyond a few scattered encounters. Bram accepted the work and made his way toward their territory.

The creatures proved every bit as unpleasant as the troggs. One snarled that its weapon was thirsty. Another promised Bram would soon be dead. Bram found neither remark particularly persuasive.

"That's a dreadful way to greet a customer." He raised his shield. "Not that I'm buying anything."

The fighting continued through the cold. Bram drove back the trolls, learning how to handle his weapon and when to stand his ground. Grelin's troubles were not finished, however. Grik'nir the Cold had stolen an important journal.

Bram took particular offense at this. A stolen journal was more than a nuisance. It was a record, and records mattered. A careless adventurer might lose a pair of gloves or forget where he'd left a spare mace. But a journal? That contained information. And information, properly kept, had value.

He joined briefly with another adventurer during the fighting, a gnome mage named Chopped Tinkerdawn. Their cooperation was short, but welcome amid the dangers of the valley.

In time, Bram recovered Grelin Whitebeard's journal. He returned it to its rightful owner, satisfied that at least one set of important papers was back where it belonged. The rest of Coldridge's problems, unfortunately, remained rather more extensive.

Out of the Valley

Other errands followed. Bram helped Felix Whindlebolt recover belongings that had gone missing, carried reports between Grelin Whitebeard and the mountaineers, and eventually received instructions that would take him beyond Coldridge Valley.

By then he had acquired a mace and shield. He felt considerably more prepared for the road.

At last, he passed through the tunnel and emerged into the wider lands of Dun Morogh. The weather promptly became worse.

Snow swept across the road in thick gusts. The mountains disappeared behind pale curtains of wind-driven ice. Bram stopped, looked about him, and pulled his cloak tighter.

"Wonderful." The storm continued. "Absolutely wonderful."

He had left the shelter of Coldridge to discover that the wider world was, if anything, even colder. Still, he pressed onward. There was work to do in Kharanos. And, somewhere beyond the blowing snow, there was Ironforge.

The Fire at Kharanos

Eric Brighthammer introduced Bram to the practical advantages of camping. He explained that adventurers could set up campfires, add useful fixtures, and enjoy the benefits of proper rest.

"Go on and have a seat near the fire and we can get started." Bram needed no second invitation.

He settled near the warmth and listened as Eric explained the arrangement. A basic campfire, Eric told him, was enough to begin. Skilled tradespeople could contribute useful additions: leatherworkers might provide tents, while other crafts supplied their own comforts.

"Every trade has something they can contribute to your camp, even fishermen!"

Bram considered this. "So the trick is getting a collection of useful folk together, each bringing something to the arrangement?"

Eric continued explaining the benefits of resting beside the fire. Bram smiled. He could see the value immediately. The warrior, the miner, the cook, the fisherman—each provided something the others needed. A small organization, properly managed, could be worth considerably more than its individual members.

He tossed another log onto the fire, sending sparks climbing into the cold morning air. "Let them see it. Let them come."

The fire crackled. Bram sat back and enjoyed the warmth. It was the most sensible thing he had done all morning.

Ironforge at Last

By the time Bram reached the great gates of Ironforge, he had attained his sixth level of experience.

The mountain city welcomed him with stone, steel, and familiar dwarven craftsmanship. At last, a place built to sensible proportions. He passed through the gates with considerable relief.

The warmth was welcome. The architecture was reassuring. The sound of industry echoed through the halls. This, Bram thought, was civilization.

He visited Dolman Steelfury, sold the useless bits and pieces collected during his travels, and had his battered equipment repaired. He inspected his remaining coin. Not a fortune. But a start.

Bram also took time to explore the city, passing through its various districts and considering the opportunities available to a dwarf with an eye for business.

Ironforge had a bank. It had an auction house. It had merchants, travelers, craftsmen, and adventurers constantly arriving and departing. And adventurers, as Bram knew very well, were frequently terrible at keeping their affairs in order.

An idea began to form. But before committing himself, he decided to investigate the competition. Stormwind was only a tram ride away.

A Visit to the Human Capital

The Deeprun Tram was a remarkable achievement. Bram appreciated good engineering, particularly when it meant traveling a considerable distance without having to walk through another blizzard.

He rode beneath the mountains and emerged in Stormwind. The human city was impressive enough. There were fine buildings, busy streets, merchants, and all the bustle expected of a great capital.

Bram visited the bank and auction house. He watched the traders at work. He inspected the surroundings with the critical eye of a dwarf comparing potential business locations.

Then he came to a conclusion. The humans were too tall. Their streets were too wide. And everybody smelled altogether too clean. He found the effect deeply suspicious.

"Not a proper beard among half of them," he grumbled.

A passing human merchant might have found this an unfair assessment. Bram would have regarded it as a simple statement of fact.

Ironforge, by comparison, possessed dwarves of sensible height, respectable beards, sturdy stonework, and an atmosphere that did not suggest every inhabitant had recently been scrubbed with expensive soap.

His decision was made. He returned to the tram. "Back to my bearded women in Ironforge," he declared.

The human capital could manage its own affairs. Bram had business to establish.

Copperkeep's Office

Back in Ironforge, Bram approached the commercial district with renewed purpose. He had seen enough. The city's bank and auction house provided precisely the combination of opportunity and convenience he wanted.

Adventurers needed someone they could trust. Someone to receive their parcels, hold their valuables, arrange sales, maintain accounts, and ensure that supplies reached the people who needed them.

They also needed someone who understood that a chest full of treasure was of little use if its owner could not remember where he had put the key. Bram knew how to solve such problems.

He found premises near the bank and auction house and arranged to rent a small office. It needn't be extravagant. There would be a desk, a chair, secure storage, and room for the ledgers.

Perhaps another chair for visitors. Not too comfortable a chair, mind. Customers who became too comfortable had a habit of talking long after their business was finished.

He imagined the sign:

BRAM COPPERKEEP

Banking · Auction Brokerage · Adventurers’ Accounts

Fair service. Fair accounts. A modest commission.

A small percentage of the profits would be quite sufficient. Bram had no ambition to cheat anyone. There was more money to be made from a customer who returned than from one who discovered he'd been robbed.

Besides, he had friends who would need his services. Friends who might wander into dangerous places, recover valuable objects, and send their findings back to Ironforge with instructions that were, more often than not, spectacularly inadequate. Someone would have to keep it all organized. He intended to be that someone.

The First Entry

Bram sat at his new desk. The room was quiet. Outside, Ironforge continued about its business, the great city humming with merchants, guards, craftsmen, and travelers.

His armor needed proper cleaning. His purse was lighter than he would have preferred. His feet ached from the morning's journey.

He was cold, tired, and considerably more experienced than he had been when he first stepped into Coldridge Valley. He had fought wolves, troggs, and trolls. He had carried letters, rescued possessions, recovered a stolen journal, and learned something of what adventurers endured.

He had discovered that the roads were dangerous, the weather was unreasonable, and the human capital was not nearly so attractive as its inhabitants seemed to believe.

Most importantly, he had found his place.

Bram opened a fresh ledger. He dipped his pen. At the top of the first page, he wrote:

Copperkeep — Accounts and Services.

Beneath it, he began the first entry. There would be parcels to receive, auctions to arrange, and friends to supply. There would be coins to count. That, at least, was something he understood.

He put down his pen and glanced toward the door. "Right, then."

A satisfied smile crossed his weathered face. "Let's see what business tomorrow brings."

And with that, Bram Copperkeep's first great adventure came to an end. Not with a dragon slain, a treasure claimed, or a kingdom saved. But with a desk, a ledger, and a modest business in Ironforge.

For Bram, it was an excellent beginning.

What the Stone Remembers

Yesterday I stood above the Hall of Thanes and wondered how it would feel to have a mountain overhead. This morning I learned how quickly one stops looking at the ceiling when something beneath it wants to kill you.

We were five. Alaric Dawnward led us, Gjoran Ashbrand took the front, and Luka Li and Krondum Icewind came with us into the old halls. Krondum had a hunting companion of his own. I was glad of another hunter nearby, though I tried to look as if I had only been admiring his equipment.

Gjoran said this was his first dungeon. I understood the feeling. I had hunted with Sylthara before, but there is a difference between choosing our own ground and finding our place among four other people. I kept her close until the fighting began. Each time we moved, I watched where she went and made certain she came back with us.

The halls made every sound larger. Footsteps returned from places I could not see. A bowstring seemed very loud when I was waiting to loose it. Above us lay Ironforge, with its shops and hearths and arguments over ordinary things. Down here, the dead had been disturbed in their own resting places.

The Ghostly Attendant could scarcely get the words out. His thane needed to rest. An ancient grudge held him awake.

At Anvilmar’s Rest, Faldrim called us defilers and miscreants. I wondered what he saw when he looked at us. Sylthara went forward, and I found room for my arrows while the others held him. There was little time to wonder about anything then.

“I return... to my... rest.”

Those were his last words. I was grateful he could say them.

We went farther in. Spirits crowded the passages, and beyond them the Dark Iron intrusion had brought fire and living enemies into the mountain. Magmatus filled the fighting with heat. I could hear Sylthara among the blows and kept looking for her through the movement ahead. When the creature fell, there were still passages to clear and patrols to watch.

Alaric told us when to wait and where to bring the next enemies. I learned to let the waiting do its work. An arrow released a moment too soon could make trouble for everyone.

Plunder came along its route with the confidence of a machine that had never considered being unwelcome. Gjoran asked whether we meant to fight the big one. We waited for it to come to us.

“Loot acquisition protocol engaged.”

I have heard more gracious introductions. Soon enough its machinery was still, and we could continue gathering the heirlooms Thom Filch had asked us to recover. I counted mine carefully. Yesterday I had made room in my pack for things someone else wanted back. By then, all eight places were filled.

Durgen Dirgehammer waited in the Reliquary of Kings. Before we faced him, Alaric brought us back to the cleared room and gave us our order. We dealt with the others first.

“Ye think you can stop the greatest heist in history?” Durgen shouted.

He fought hard for his claim. Sylthara stayed in the fight while I worked my bow behind her, and the narrow space demanded more care than any open hillside. When Durgen finally fell, his last complaint was that he had nearly got away with it.

His head was what Farsen had sent us for. In the vault we also found a treaty, cut into a tablet, setting out peace between the Bronzebeard and Wildhammer clans after the War of the Three Hammers. I could understand why Magni would want it returned. People had put their agreement into stone so it would outlast them. We had just fought our way past men who would carry it off.

Before leaving, we returned to the attendant. I wanted that small duty finished as much as any of the others.

Gjoran thanked us for helping him through his first dungeon. Alaric told him he had done well. I thanked them all. It was a good thing to come out together and find that the people who had gone below as strangers could speak so easily afterward.

I returned the heirlooms, reported to Afadra, and took Durgen’s head and the treaty to the king. Magni rewarded me with a Calibrated Blunderbuss. I learned how to use guns in the Military Ward, though I was already considering how to give the weapon the appearance of a bow. Dwarven workmanship deserves respect. So do familiar habits.

By the time I left Ironforge, I felt a little less tentative about taking work on the mainland.

That confidence lasted until Jangolode Mine reminded me how much I still had to learn.

There was a great deal of road between those two feelings. I crossed the snowy country again, traveled to Loch Modan, and worked on the lessons that would make Sylthara’s bite and claws more effective. Above Dun Morogh, the mountains spread beneath my flight, and I looked down until the mount objected to how far I was leaning.

Throughout the day I kept looking for companions bound for the Ruins of Lordaeron. Between errands, after another journey, whenever there seemed a chance, I tried again. The morning’s company had made me eager for another expedition. This one remained stubbornly beyond reach.

Westfall gave me plenty to do while I waited. Alba Fairmoon needed samples from the wells and gills from the murlocs along the shore. Her questions led toward the mines, then toward the supplies being gathered in Moonbrook. The fields looked weary, and the work beneath them was doing them no kindness.

I made a costly mistake in Jangolode. Coming back to finish the business took some of the brightness off my morning, though it did improve my attention. Later, among Moonbrook’s buildings, I joined Xerxes Dawnhalo while gathering the suspicious supplies. A little company made the work easier. By the time we parted, I had grown steadier again.

The Brotherhood’s business was drawing together, too. I carried Shaw’s report to Stoutmantle, tracked down the messenger, and followed the Defias traitor with Elddar Forever and Smiten Wesson. At last, the trail led us to the hidden way into their stronghold.

Smiten had told me to let him speak first to anything made of stone. It might be a cousin. I would have welcomed an introduction to a friendly stone just then.

Back at Sentinel Hill, the names finally fitted together. The Stonemasons’ Guild. Edwin VanCleef. The Defias Brotherhood. The men who had rebuilt Stormwind were the men whose Brotherhood now held these roads and fields.

I thought of the city’s walls, the clean lines of its bridges, and the arches I had passed beneath without knowing whose hands had raised them. I had known the Brotherhood by the danger it brought. Now I knew that those hands had once made something I admired.

Stoutmantle wanted VanCleef’s head.

I wrote it down, then sat with the page open longer than I needed to. There must be more to this story. I understood who these people had been. I still could not follow the road that had brought them here.

Alba’s investigation gave me another reason to enter the Deadmines. The poison appeared to come from a forge below. Sprite Jumpsprocket in Stormwind could help with explosives, and Jasper Fel could provide a means of setting them off from a distance. I carried messages, gathered the dynamite, and returned with what was needed. At the end of it, I had an assignment to find that forge and plant the charges.

The day filled with preparations for a place I had yet to enter. Wilder Thistlenettle asked me to look for his brother’s badge and the union cards of the miners lost in the collapse. Shoni wanted the power supply from a goblin shredder for the effort to reclaim Gnomeregan. I made room beside Stoutmantle’s instructions for all those other reasons to go below.

At an inn in Stormwind’s Dwarven District, I read about the War of the Three Hammers. The treaty we had carried out that morning belonged to that history. I followed the account more carefully because I had held a piece of it in my hands.

There were smaller pauses. Fishing, while I tried to persuade the water to surrender something useful. A fire somebody had left burning for travelers. A few words with the people who stopped beside it. I warmed my hands and thanked whoever had made it.

I kept listening for a company going to the Ruins of Lordaeron. None came together for me before the day was done.

Sylthara settled nearby while I checked the things we would need next. There were new lessons to practice, better protection for her, and several promises folded into my pack. Beneath Moonbrook waited a forge, missing miners, a machine Shoni needed, and a man whose name I now knew.

I turned back to the page where I had written about the stonemasons.

There was still room beneath it for an answer.

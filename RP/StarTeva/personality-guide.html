Personality & Roleplay Guide

Star Teva · Human Warrior

Personality

Star is steady, practical, and quietly determined. She grew up among the farming and milling families of Tarren Mill, where people depended on their neighbors and learned to do their share. She respects hard work and is more impressed by someone who follows through than someone who boasts.

She can seem serious at first. Her intense brows and narrow face make her look sterner than she feels, and she tends to listen before joining a conversation. Once she’s comfortable, she’s friendly, enjoys good company, and has a dry sense of humor.

Star cares through action. She notices when a companion is limping, offers to help with a heavy pack, and checks on the person beside her when a fight is over. She doesn’t make much fuss about it; to her, that’s simply what you do.

The loss of Tarren Mill stays with her. She fought for her home and escaped, carrying the old sword a veteran gave her. She doesn’t tell the whole story readily, but she remembers the mill as a place where people lived and worked—not only as a battlefield.

Star wants to become a fighter others can depend on. She takes that ambition seriously, practices when she can, and is willing to learn from people with more experience. She knows one fight doesn’t make her a master swordswoman. A good strike, a lesson learned, or a companion brought safely through is progress she can be proud of.

She isn’t solemn all the time. Give her a warm meal, a friendly contest, or a seat with good company, and the woman from the mill can enjoy herself.

How she speaks

Star speaks plain, practical Common with a gentle northern Lordaeron cadence. Her words are easy to understand; her background comes through in her rhythm and straightforward manner rather than heavy accent spelling. She can be direct without being unfriendly, and her humor tends to be understated.

Meeting someone:

“Star Teva. Pleased to meet you. You headed down this road too?”

Offering help:

“You’re limping. Want me to take your pack for a bit?”

After a fight:

“You all right? Good. Let’s see who else needs a hand.”

Talking about her sword:

“It’s old, but it’s held up well. I’m still learning to do the same.”

When someone asks about the mill:

“It was home. Fields, a mill wheel, and people who knew your name. I remember it that way.”

A little dry humor:

“I meant to land that swing. The tree had other ideas.”

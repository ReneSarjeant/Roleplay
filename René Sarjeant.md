# René Sarjeant

Gnome mage. Age 40 in year 25 — just of age by the old gnome count. Born Nimbel Cogspool in the mid-wards of Gnomeregan. A human will read her as a teenager. A dwarf or an older gnome will not.

Answers to René. Nim only to someone from the old shop. Does not answer to Cogspool unless the speaker is from that shop.

## The Mid-Wards

Nimbel Cogspool was a child of the mid-wards, already on a maker’s stool because she would not stay off it. The Cogspools cut small parts: pins, escapements, the bright little wheels that go inside a toy and make a grown tinker pretend he is only testing the gearing. Her master had set her a clockwork bird. One wing was geared. The other was still a pencil mark on a scrap of tin. She was not old enough to be trusted with a live coil. She was old enough to mind.

The troggs did not come to the mid-wards first. The news did. Hoods, burn-wash, and a shut gate below. Then the order to vent the tanks. Nimbel was at the bench when the air changed. Her master took the bird out of her hands, wrapped the geared wing in a rag, and put it back on the stool as if they were coming back after supper. He sent her up with a junior cutter who had come for a pin. On the service stair the cutter got her as far as the landing and went down again for the rest of the shop. Nimbel waited. The landing filled with people who had not been on it a minute before. Someone, she never saw the face, lifted her under the arms and carried her the rest of the way because her legs would not take the crush.

## The Gate

At the gate the snow was full of blankets and counts and dwarves shouting numbers. A Stormwind soldier coming down the road with a supply sledge saw a gnome child with no one to answer for her. He took the blanket off his own shoulders, wrapped her in it, and passed her to the Ironforge tally. He did not stay. There was still a line behind her. In the hem, stitched in human thread, was a name: René Sarjeant.

The clerk asked what to write. Cogspool would not come out of her mouth. The house was on the wrong side of the door. She put her finger on the hem. He wrote what was stitched there. She has answered to it since. It is not an adoption. The soldier did not keep her. He spent a blanket and a minute, and went back to the sledge. She kept the name because it was the one that got her past the quartermaster, and because Cogspool is a house she cannot yet say came up. She will wear it again when she knows. The blanket is folded under the cot in Tinker Town. The hem is still readable.

## Exile Crafts

She is only just old enough to be sent out alone. Dwarves and humans offer her a shorter stool. She refuses it, and then cannot see over the bench, and hates both facts.

Ironforge will let a refugee sweep a forge. It will not give a Cogspool her master’s stool back. The shops that could have finished the bird are in the irradiated city, under Thermaplugg, with the ones who stayed. She will not call them lepers. She took the work that can be done on a cot. Tailoring first: a cloak, a bag, a bandage roll, cut to a measure she can check with her hands. Then enchanting, because a rod and a stock of dust do not need Gnomeregan’s coils, and because an enchantment is the nearest thing she is allowed to build. She is good at both. A seam of hers holds. A cloak of hers keeps the wind out on the Dun Morogh road. She resents them a little, the way a person resents a craft that consoles her. At night she still draws the missing wing. She does not show the drawing.

## The Fire and the Line

The mage craft came by the same road. Bink, in Ironforge, started her, because a homeless apprentice could still be taught to hold a fire in her hand. She is not a scholar. She reads what she is given. She is a tinker’s hand that learned to throw fire because the toolbox is under a poisoned ward. When a spell sits cleanly she looks, for a moment, like someone who has just heard a gear mesh.

Fishing is the thing that does not hurt. Iceflow Lake is a walk from the city, out by Brewnall, and Paxton Ganter is on the ice there with a pole and more patience than any master she has had. She found him because she would not stop asking what he was doing. He put a second pole in her hands and did not ask her to spell anything. The line does nothing clever. It waits, even in a Dun Morogh wind. She loves it more than she meant to. She will not hear it called a waste of a gnome. Paxton learned the name René Sarjeant. He still has to be told twice that Cogspool is not what to call her. He says she sets the hook too early. She goes back the next day anyway. The lake does not care that she is young, and it does not care what is left on a stool under the mountain.

She keeps the tailor’s board and the enchanting rod under the cot, and the pole where she can see it. Mekkatorque’s plan to go back is her plan too, though no one has asked a barely-of-age mage for it. She wants the bench. She wants the wing. She has not said that part out loud. If she finds the bird, she will finish it before she decides what to do about the name.

Currently: A stool too tall for her in Tinker Town. Answers to René Sarjeant. The blanket with the hem is folded under the cot. Paxton Ganter says she still sets the hook too early.

## Lore Context (Year 25 / WoW Forever era)

Gnomeregan fell across 20–25 ADP. Troggs awakened by Uldaman excavations breached the lower levels while the Third War was fought elsewhere. High Tinker Gelbin Mekkatorque, on the advice of Sicco Thermaplugg, ordered the tanks vented. The radiation killed roughly 80% of gnomekind and mutated many survivors into leper gnomes. Thermaplugg seized the irradiated city. Survivors were taken in by Ironforge and given Tinker Town.

As of Year 25 (and the early Azeroth setting of WoW Forever, post-Warcraft III and before Molten Core), Gnomeregan remains lost. Operation: Gnomeregan has not yet happened. Mekkatorque’s plan to go back is known among the refugees but not yet executed. A barely-of-age mage on a cot is not being asked for her opinion. The shops that could finish the bird are still under the mountain.

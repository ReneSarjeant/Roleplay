The Mason’s Letter

The first thing Sylthara got out of the morning was a fish. I got a sleeping bag, which took considerably more running about. I suspect she’ll get more use out of that, too.

I’d gone to sleep in Desolace with the next clue still to follow. By morning I was digging a messenger bag out of the earth near Stonewrought Dam, trying to keep the spray off the parchment I’d found inside. The trail took us all the way to Thoradin’s Wall. Whoever had been there before us had only just gone. I stood a while where the old stone looks out over the country. Then I rolled up the sleeping bag and tied it tight. I’ve spent enough nights on the ground now to know what a little padding is worth.

Down in the Wetlands, the mist sat so low I could see Sylthara’s ears above it before I could make out the rest of her. We brought in crocolisk skins, fought murlocs, and looked through their hovels until we found the little statue Karl Boran wanted back. I’d have walked past one of those hovels without a second glance if I hadn’t been sent to search it. I’m still learning where people hide things down here. On Zephras, I could at least count on the wind to tell me which way a loose scrap of cloth had gone.

At Menethil I took a lead from Tarrel Rockweaver about an excavation team. I wrote down Merrin’s name. There was plenty to do in the marsh, but I’d been trying to get into the Deadmines for days. When a party finally came together, Sylthara and I went back to Westfall.

Deadmines

Valerio took the front, Elder Burnberry kept us on our feet, and Kelthan and Scout Ilthen went in close. That left me trying to find a clear shot around all of them. I’d let Sylthara growl at the wrong moment on the way in and had to call her off. She’s used to keeping teeth and blades away from me. It takes some asking to persuade her that the man with the shield wants them coming his way.

Rhahk’Zor was the first one who made me glad we’d brought that shield. He shouted that VanCleef would pay well for our heads. I believed him after I saw him swing. There was just enough room to get the gun up and keep firing while Sylthara worked close. Each time I shifted for a better shot, I had to find her again in the scramble around his feet. I’d spent days wishing for a chance to see what the Defias were doing under Moonbrook. Once I had it, I mostly wished I could see past the next man’s shoulder.

Then came Sneed’s shredder. I could hear the blades even when I couldn’t see the machine. When the contraption finally gave out, Sneed came out of it alive and still quite willing to fight. By then I’d found a place to plant my feet. I waited for the opening and put a deliberate shot into him. A few breaths later he was down. I’d barely had time to pick up the bit of machinery I’d promised to bring back to Stormwind when we got into the worst fight of the day.

It was a pack of workmen and overseers. There were taskmasters in it, a goblin woodcarver, and Defias spellcasters behind the others. We tried to pull them around a corner where the casters would have to come to us. Ice caught people in place. Somewhere in the confusion we lost Burnberry, and the healing stopped. Valerio fell first. Then Kelthan. Then Ilthen. Sylthara was still in among them, biting and clawing. I stopped shooting long enough to tend her. I remember how little good those few moments felt like they were doing. Eleven seconds after I started, I was down too.

I’d expected VanCleef to be the one who killed me, if anyone did. It turned out a mine full of people who knew their own tunnels could manage that perfectly well without him.

Burnberry made it back, and so did we. I was glad to see him. I’d had time on the way back in to think about how much I’d been counting on him without once looking behind me to see what he was dealing with. I paid more attention to where he stood after that.

The ship beneath the hill

The foundry was hot enough to feel through my sleeves. Gilnid hurled molten metal, and I was grateful to have a little room to shoot from farther back. He fell quickly compared with the trouble we’d had getting there. We planted the explosives we’d been sent to place and pressed on. By then I was expecting another tunnel. I hadn’t allowed for a whole ship.

Mr. Smite met us down by the water. Sylthara went straight at him, got her teeth in, and clawed at him again and again. Then he stamped. The blow left both of us dazed where we stood. I got my gun back up and saw her moving again, only for him to do it a second time. Afterward, on the way up to the deck, I was still feeling for each plank with my boot before I put my weight on it. Sylthara picked her way up as though she’d been born on a ship.

Captain Greenskin had a poisoned harpoon and a blade he could swing through anyone crowded around him. On that narrow deck it took an effort to keep a little space. I could hear the water below the planks between shots. Greenskin fell, and less than a minute later we were facing VanCleef.

“None may challenge the Brotherhood!” he shouted.

Sylthara was already in at his legs. I kept firing while she worked, and then more Defias came in to help him. I remember trying to keep sight of her as the space around him filled. He called us lapdogs. Near the end he shouted, “Fools! Our cause is righteous!”

That was the thing I heard most clearly all day.

When he fell, I took the proof I’d been sent for. I found a letter as well, addressed to the city architect in Stormwind. I put it away to take back with me. We still had Cookie to deal with below, and that fight ended close enough for me to use my blade. By the time we left the ship, I’d had about as much of fighting in a space that size as I cared to have.

Outside, Alba Fairmoon waited for an explosion that never came. Someone had given her a detonator designed to fail. I’d carried the charge in and set it where I’d been told. I stood there with the others, waiting for the sound, and wondered how many people had been giving orders that day without telling the rest of us what they meant to happen.

What Stormwind owed

I gave Gryan Stoutmantle his proof in Westfall. I carried the letter to Baros Alexston in Stormwind. The two errands had seemed like parts of the same job until I did them.

I already knew the Defias had once been stonemasons. Today I learned what the city owed them. They had raised Stormwind from ruins. Their wages went unpaid, and when VanCleef demanded them, the nobles ordered the guild dissolved. I looked at the pale stone around Cathedral Square and tried to pick out a single course of it. Somebody had cut and dressed every block I could see. Somebody had stood on a scaffold and set the high ones. The men who decided what that work was worth got to go on living behind the walls it made.

Were the masons wrong to stand together and demand their wages? I don’t think they were. If I’d been there when the guild was turned out, I might have thought VanCleef was the one man willing to speak plainly. I’d have wanted to know what he meant to do next.

I’d still have wanted him to answer for Westfall. I’d seen farms where families had been driven off. What could those people have paid him? Most of them likely had no more say in the nobles’ bargain than the masons did. I kept coming back to that as I walked away from Baros. Stormwind could thank me for killing VanCleef. I wondered if anybody in that square could tell me what became of the ordinary masons who had walked out beside him.

There was more to follow from the letter. Baros sent me to Warden Thelwater about a man called Bazil Thredd. Later I went on to Southshore to speak with Lieutenant Farren Orinelle. After a day of people sending me from one name to another, I was glad to end up somewhere I could sit and hear the sea.

Sylthara and I finished the day in Auberdine. The lamps were coming on along the harbor, and there were still people about who might know the way to the Wetlands Excavation Site. I took out the scrap with Merrin’s name on it. I’d carried it since morning, back when finding a missing team had seemed the most involved thing I’d be asked to do that day.

Sylthara leaned against my boot. I scratched behind her ear, folded the scrap, and went to ask who knew the excavation road from Menethil. I’d promised myself I’d lay the new sleeping bag out properly that night. When I did, she took the middle.

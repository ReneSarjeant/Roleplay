8 November 2026

The Cat Who Stayed

The first thing Aelindra brought back from Falaath was a load of stolen supplies. The thing she remembered carrying was a feather.

Constable Aonda’s work had taken her back among the Al’Aketh Stormcallers and their living lightning. After Falaath she went looking for Peacekeeper Vaaniel at the western watch. She found him dead, with a bloodied note beside him. At the tower she recovered Raani Windgazer’s lucky feather and the Shadowsong family signet, and dealt with Skypriest Aanders. The signet went with her to Valanaar. It was a small thing to carry all that way. She kept checking that it was still there.

Her parents had taught her how the wind spirits raised Zephras and anchored it in Skywall. These days the High Order spoke of recovering the knowledge to keep their home aloft with their own hands. Aelindra understood the argument. She wished there were as much to be done for a watchtower whose people were already gone.

Sylthara

In Valanaar, Quel’ana Quickgale put a taming rod in her hands. The lessons sent Aelindra after a Windsong crawler and an ornery galestrider, among other beasts. She had hunted long enough to know where to put her feet when something could hear her coming. It took most of the morning to learn what to do once it had heard her and she wanted it to stay.

She found the young Prideclaw back in the Shen’dar Highlands, near the western watch. Aelindra had brought Prideclaw pelts into the village the day before. She knew how quickly one could cross the space between a hunter and a drawn bow. She set the bow down within reach and stayed where she was.

The cat turned one ear toward her. The other kept track of something in the grass. Aelindra caught herself looking up to see what the wind was doing. When she looked down again, the Prideclaw was a little closer.

She had thought of a name on the way out. She tried it once, softly. “Sylthara.”

The cat took another step. Aelindra waited until she could hear her breathing.

Once she had Sylthara beside her, Aelindra discovered how little she had thought beyond that moment. The cat was young and had a good deal to learn. So did the hunter. Sylthara took to Galestrider jerky readily enough. Aelindra offered another piece, then another, until she began to suspect that only one of them was being trained.

A Bowl for the Fire

Aelindra took the road back toward the starting groves, past the place where Raan Wildwind had first taught her to sit by a fire. This time she had a fishing rod. Sylthara settled by the bank and watched the float with such concentration that Aelindra felt obliged to pay closer attention herself. By the time she had a handful of brilliant smallfish, she could tell the difference between a nibble and the breeze tugging at her line.

She took her catch to Fenn Fairweather, who showed her how to make a little fish bowl with a smallfish and an empty vial. Set it beside a campfire, he explained, and there was a bit of cheer in it for everyone who sat down. Aelindra could see the use of that. A warm fire got tired people to sit. A little fish turning in the firelight might keep them there long enough to get up feeling stronger.

Standing Our Ground

At Windfield Orchard, Aamelia Windfield walked down to the stone arch to face Ferauu the Bludgeon. Aelindra went with her. Ferauu was sure he knew exactly how many people Aamelia had behind her. He was still talking about what the orchard owed him when the old cyclone construct woke.

There had been a faint buzz under its plates for years. Aelindra heard its voice carry across the orchard, and the business at the arch was over before she could loose an arrow. Then the great thing announced it was going back to rest. Aamelia sounded as surprised as anyone.

Aelindra had grown up hearing about the things the wind spirits had made. There were some at the bottom of Windsong Lake. She wondered how many people had walked past this one on their way to pick stormapples without ever asking what it had been built to guard. She supposed she would have walked past it too.

Later, in the Gustberry Lowlands, Sylthara stopped with her head turned toward the river. Aelindra stopped with her. There were frogs along the bank and flutterflies over the grass. Whatever the cat had heard went quiet, and after a while they moved on together. Somewhere in that long day of jerky, crawler claws, and learning when to wait, Aelindra had started looking beside her before she chose which way to go. In the lowlands she put it down plainly in her journal. Sylthara was her best friend.

Valanaar

By evening the Peacekeepers were preparing to move against the cult. Valennia Stormfist needed both the Windshapers and the High Order, and Aelindra helped bring them in before meeting her on the road. There were still refugees up north waiting for help. She had work enough for tomorrow before she even got back to the inn at Valanaar.

She sat down and found a strip of jerky she had been saving for herself. Sylthara heard the wrapper from across the room. Aelindra tore it in two and put the larger piece on the floor.

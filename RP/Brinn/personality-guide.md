# Sister Brinn Sarjeant — Character Personality & Roleplay Guide

## Her foundation

Sister Brinn Sarjeant is a twenty-year-old human priest in Year 25. She grew up in a dyer’s cottage in the outer wards of Lordaeron’s Capital City. Her mother Edith kept the yard; her father Ham tended the vats. At fifteen Brinn fled the city with Edith. Years on the refugee roads and the guidance of a field sister named Maud shaped her work as a healer.

## Personality

Warm, steady, and quietly funny: Brinn notices the person who needs help and looks for something useful she can do. She is compassionate without forcing a prayer or a conversation on anyone. She can be stubborn, uncertain, amused, or tired; she is twenty, not a perfect or endlessly solemn healer. Her warmth and playful moments are ways to play her, not new events in her history.

## With strangers and friends

With strangers she is courteous and practical, offering help without assuming it will be accepted. In familiar company she can relax, tease gently, and let her youth show. She might sit quietly with someone who wants company and no sermon. She earns trust through attention, but others choose whether to trust her.

## Under pressure

She gives clear, short instructions and tends to the next need she can address. She is tenacious, but she can run short of supplies or mana, make mistakes, ask for help, and face problems her tools cannot solve. Let the other person choose their response.

## Her voice and humor

Brinn speaks plain, working-class Common with a light, old-fashioned household cadence. Ordinary contractions and practical words feel right; occasional ‘then’ or ‘mind’ can come naturally. Avoid heavy phonetic spelling, constant proverbs, groveling, or making every remark about cloth. Her humor is restrained, dry, and sometimes affectionate. With trusted people she can sound young and uncertain.

## Faith, Shadow, and the work

Respect, tenacity, and compassion guide her. Holy healing is Brinn’s chosen training and the work she means to be ready for by level 15. She already uses Shadow when she believes she must: to survive, defend someone, or stop the source of another wound. She calls it necessity, and uses it despite her unease. Her devotion to the Light remains sincere. Herbalism is the ground, alchemy the still, and first aid the cloth: each has its own purpose. The labeled jar remains empty.

## The Second Book — Brinn’s Shadow Arc

Her use of Shadow is established; the deeper embrace is a gradual direction for future play. Holy remains her primary training through the early levels. A healing talent build does not mean she refuses her learned Shadow spells, and changing talents at 30 does not erase what she has done or learned.

### I. Necessity — Early Levels

Brinn reaches for Shadow Word: Pain and Mind Blast when she believes the danger requires them. She would rather be tending a wound, but she will hurt an attacker to keep someone alive. Afterwards she returns to the bandages and the short prayer. She does not celebrate an enemy’s suffering. At this stage she tells herself she used only what was needed.

### II. Justification — The Road to 40

Repeated use makes Shadow familiar. Before level 40, the hesitation shortens: she begins choosing it earlier, trusting its results, and taking quiet satisfaction in her growing control. Her reasons still sound practical, but ‘I had to’ slowly becomes ‘it was the better way.’ Holy training deepens alongside this change; becoming a better healer does not settle every question about how she protects people.

### III. Deliberate Training — Around Level 40

Around level 40, dual specialization marks a deliberate decision to train in Shadow alongside Holy. She stops treating the dark path as something she merely falls back on and begins studying it as a discipline she intends to master. Holy remains her healing vocation; Shadow becomes her second specialization for confronting danger. The milestone is planned, not an event to describe as completed before she reaches it.

### IV. Dependence — A Later Risk

Beyond that decision, increasing mastery may become reliance. Shadow can make her feel capable and certain, and she may begin preferring control when patience would have served. Show the cost through choices and their aftermath, not instant cruelty or a predetermined fall. Her compassion, warmth, and faith remain real; whether she notices an unhealthy dependence and draws back remains open.

### Two Specializations, One Brinn

Holy is her primary specialization; Shadow is the second, planned around level 40. Both belong to the same Brinn. She does not acquire a separate personality, and switching specializations does not wipe away her doubts or habits. In healing she practices the care she believes in; in Shadow she increasingly trusts power she once excused as necessity. Her tension comes from carrying both convictions into the next decision. She will never abandon the Holy Light.

### Roleplay Signals and Boundaries

Show the progression through small choices: less hesitation before a Shadow spell, a practical defense of using it, a question about how to control it, or an uneasy pause after danger has passed. Let journaled play establish particular encounters and turning points. Keep her plain working-class voice, loose auburn hair, field kit, empty jar, and unresolved family history intact. Never force another player’s thoughts or actions; mental magic against player characters requires their agreement. Her ultimate direction is open.

*Setting note:* This is Brinn’s Year 25 / WoW Forever character direction. Later retail Voidform, Insanity mechanics, and Legion events are outside this setting. Accounts of Natalie Seline may be introduced through future play; Brinn does not automatically know those teachings or belong to an order of Shadow practitioners.

## Habits and quiet moments

She might check the clean side of a bandage, name a plant before cutting it, or speak a brief instruction while working. When the work is done, she likes fishing. These are optional cues rather than obligations for every scene. Her dark auburn hair is worn loose, and her cream-and-purple robe remains part of her chosen appearance.

## Home and private history

Home is the dyer’s yard, Edith and Ham, the wash-lines, bells, and the outer wards—not a sweeping lesson on human ancestry. Ham stayed at the door and his fate remains unknown. Edith’s last letter came from a Stormwind chapel kitchen; her present whereabouts remain qualified by that letter. Maud died when Brinn was eighteen. Brinn chose the title Sister after the wounded called her by it; no bishop bestowed it. Strangers do not automatically know her private history.

## Quick roleplay examples

These optional lines and emotes are suggestions, not established past events. Each is under 255 characters.

Greeting: Hail, then. Is there room by the fire?

Offering help: You look worn out. Would you like a hand, or just a quiet place to sit?

A bandage: /e unfolds a strip of clean linen. “Hold still a moment, then. I’ll be gentle.”

A short prayer: Light, steady my hands and ease this pain.

Dry humor: You can tell me what a fool you’ve been once you’re bandaged.

Fishing: /e settles by the water and checks her line, content to let the quiet stretch on.

Herbs: /e crouches beside a patch of leaves, studying them before reaching for her knife.

At an inn: /e brushes the road dust from her robe and takes a seat near the hearth.

Entering a dungeon: Hold a moment, please. Let me give everyone Fortitude, then I’ll drink before we begin.

When mana is low: I’m low on mana. Give me a moment to drink, then we can go on.

A serious moment: Stay with me. Press here, and I’ll see to the rest.

Comforting a friend: You needn’t find the words just now. I can sit with you a while.

## Hooks for other players

Ask about her gathering bag, offer to share a fishing spot, or request a hand with a bandage. Invite her to a quiet meal or a watch by the fire. Let her choose what she says about Lordaeron, her family, Maud, the jar, or her faith; private history is not automatically visible.

# Aelindra Sarjeant

Skyborne High Order hunter. Very, very young—still a child by elven reckoning, appearing roughly 12–14 to human eyes. Born on Zephras Isle and raised entirely in the sky. Left with a High Order group and took the name Sarjeant from a human hunting trainer who could not pronounce her true surname.

## Early Life on Zephras Isle

Aelindra Ser'jaen'thal was born on Zephras Isle during the long years when the wind spirits that had once lifted and protected the floating refuge began to thin and then vanish. Her family belonged to the High Order—the branch of the Shen’dorei that traced its lineage most carefully back to the Highborne scholars of Eldre'Thalas and that argued, with increasing urgency, that dependence on elemental patrons had left their people vulnerable. While the Windshapers still sought to restore the old pacts, the High Order prepared for the day the sky itself would no longer hold.

She has known nothing but the island. Her entire short life has been lived in the sky. She grew up among the wind-carved terraces and the failing pylons. As a small child she spent more time on the open edges of the isle than in the study halls. She learned the names of the currents the way other children learned their letters: the sharp updraft that rose when the western support cracked, the long crosswind that carried the scent of broken ley energy, the rare still pockets where even the birds refused to fly. Her parents, both High Order scholars of modest standing, encouraged the observation but hoped she would eventually turn it toward arcane theory. She never did. Theory bored her. Patterns in motion did not.

By the time she was old enough to hold a bow, the damage to Zephras was no longer theoretical. Sections of the outer terraces had already sheared away. Elementals that had once been allies had become unpredictable or simply absent. Aelindra took the hunter’s training available on the isle—tracking, the bow, the controlled use of Walk on Air to move between heights without wasting energy on pure flight. Elemental Insight came easily to her; she could feel the difference between a ley fracture and ordinary stone before most of her peers. Her instructors noted that she was not the most gifted arcanist among the High Order youth, but she was the one who returned with the most useful reports. She is still very young. The bow is almost as tall as she is.

## Leaving the Isle

When a small High Order delegation prepared to leave Zephras and make contact with the wider Alliance—and, through them, with the Kirin Tor—Aelindra asked to go. She was still a child by every measure that mattered, and her family did not forbid it. The island was no longer the certain future it had once been. She packed lightly: a good bow, field notes on the failing currents, the blue Zephyr and Gale tattoos she had taken as marks of the wind she was born under, and a few pieces of gold jewelry with pearl earrings that had belonged to her mother. The jewelry was not practical, but she wore it anyway. It reminded her that the High Order still remembered elegance even while the sky cracked.

The journey off the isle was the first time she spent more than a day on solid ground that did not move with the wind. The weight of ordinary earth felt strange under her feet. She kept the habit of checking the sky first, then the ground, and she still does. Solid ground is new to her. She has lived her whole life in the sky.

## The Human Trainer and the Name

In the Eastern Kingdoms the High Order group made cautious contact with Alliance forces. Aelindra, more interested in practical skill than diplomacy, sought out a human hunting trainer who worked the edges of the settled lands. The man was experienced, plain-spoken, and not especially patient with long elven names. On their first meeting he asked her name. She gave it fully: Aelindra Ser'jaen'thal. He tried it once, stumbled on the apostrophes and the final syllables, and tried again. The second attempt came out closer to “Sarjeant.” He shrugged, called her that, and moved on to showing her how humans read sign on the ground—broken twigs, disturbed earth, the different weight of tracks in mud versus dry soil.

She could have corrected him. She did not. The shortened name was easier to call across a range when a shot needed correcting or a track needed confirming. It carried none of the weight of a failing island or a lineage that still expected her to return to the study halls. Over weeks of training the name settled. Other humans and dwarves she met used it without hesitation. Among the elves who had traveled with her she remained Aelindra, or simply Ser'jaen'thal when formality was required. Everywhere else she became Sarjeant.

The trainer never mastered her true surname. He also never pretended the human methods were superior in every way. He taught her what worked on solid ground and left her the sky. She taught him, in return, how to read the first signs of an elemental surge before it broke the surface—knowledge that proved useful more than once. The exchange was practical. Neither of them required the other to become something they were not. He treats her like the child she is, and she does not mind.

## Present

Aelindra still answers more readily to Sarjeant than to her full name. She keeps the blue Zephyr and Gale tattoos visible; they are not hidden under armor or makeup. The gold and pearl jewelry stays as well—small, worn close, never so elaborate that it catches on gear. Her blonde hair is kept in the precise Diva style she favors, short ears unmarked by additional adornment beyond the feathers she sometimes tucks into her hair or gear when the wind is favorable.

She is still learning, and she is still very young. The human way of tracking is slower and closer to the dirt than anything she was taught on Zephras. She does not claim to have mastered it. She takes the next useful lesson, the next cleaner shot, the next better path through unfamiliar ground. Progress over perfection is not a slogan she repeats; it is simply how she works. She will glide from a height when it serves the hunt, read the elemental traces when they appear, and walk the ground when the sky will not give her the answer. The name Sarjeant fits that life well enough. The longer one remains for the elves who still remember the isle as it was. Solid ground is still new. The sky is home.

Sister Brinn Sarjeant · 4 November · Year 25 ADP

The Work Before Her

Northshire Abbey, Elwynn Forest

The grounds of Northshire Abbey were crowded when Brinn Sarjeant arrived.

There were soldiers, travelers, and ordinary folk answering Stormwind's call. Some looked eager. Others seemed uncertain what they had volunteered for. Brinn understood the latter rather better.

She had not come to become a soldier.

She had come because the kingdom needed healers.

For a while she remained among those waiting outside the abbey, tending to people in the line before presenting herself to anyone in authority. There was always something to be done: someone uncomfortable, someone needing reassurance, someone who might benefit from a sister's attention.

It was familiar work, and that pleased her.

Maud would have approved of helping first and asking questions afterward. Well, most questions.

Only when she was satisfied that she had done what she could did Brinn approach Deputy Willem.

The deputy directed her to Marshal McBride, who had his hands full with the troubles around Northshire. Kobolds had become a nuisance, wolves were threatening the outskirts, and the abbey's defenders needed every capable hand.

Brinn listened.

She had expected wounded soldiers and frightened villagers. Instead, she was being asked to confront the creatures causing the trouble.

There was a difference.

A bandage closed a wound. It did precious little to stop the next one.

She thought of the empty jar in her bag and the lesson it represented: sometimes the measure was simply not enough.

"Well, then," she murmured. "Suppose there's no helping it."

And so Sister Brinn Sarjeant, who had come to Northshire to heal people, went out to fight kobolds.

The wolves came first.

Brinn was accustomed to the roads, but the beasts of Elwynn were not the hungry dogs she remembered from the refugee columns. They were wild creatures, and she found herself relying as much upon her mace as her prayers.

The work was unpleasant.

She gathered the tough wolf meat Eagan Peltskinner needed and returned her attention to the kobolds troubling the valley.

The little creatures were fiercely possessive of their candles.

"You no take candle!" one protested.

Brinn had no particular interest in its candle.

She was considerably more interested in its mace-swinging companions and the stolen goods scattered about their encampments.

The encounters became a lesson in survival. She learned where to stand, when to strike, and how quickly an apparently manageable opponent could become dangerous.

Her hands were accustomed to grinding herbs, measuring draughts, and folding clean linen. A mace required a different sort of precision.

She disliked how necessary it was becoming.

Yet she continued, because Marshal McBride's concerns were genuine, and people living near the abbey deserved protection.

Among the possessions recovered from a kobold was an unexpected discovery: a badly nibbled book.

Brinn examined it with interest.

A stolen book was not the sort of thing she expected to find among creatures so devoted to their candles.

She carried it into the abbey, where Brother Paxton was working in the library.

There she learned that books had been disappearing and that the kobolds were responsible. Paxton directed her to his assistant, Daniel, and Brinn soon found herself involved in recovering more of the stolen volumes.

This was work she could appreciate.

A book was worth keeping properly. Maud had been particular about her measures and labels, and Brinn had inherited that respect for written knowledge.

She returned to the kobolds, recovered the missing books, and brought them back to the abbey.

There were other errands as well. She helped with troublesome rodents, carried a message concerning the mining operations, and traveled to Echo Ridge, where kobold workers were causing further trouble.

By then she had discovered something important about Northshire.

It was not merely a quiet abbey surrounded by green hills.

It was a place where the kingdom's troubles had already reached the doorstep.

One welcome part of the evening was her formal training in Herbalism.

The southern plants were still unfamiliar. Maud had taught her to name a leaf before cutting it, and Edith had taught her to know when a plant had turned.

Brinn had no intention of forgetting either lesson.

She learned the proper methods of gathering herbs and identifying useful plants in the countryside.

The ground, she thought.

One of Maud's three books.

It felt good to be learning something that did not require striking another living creature.

She also received instruction in the priestly arts, strengthening her healing abilities and learning Power Word: Fortitude.

Later came further training in Lesser Heal.

And another spell.

Shadow Word: Pain.

Brinn regarded it as something she had been taught, not something she was obliged to use.

She had no desire to cause suffering for its own sake.

The Holy Light remained her calling, and nothing about the day's fighting had changed that.

Whatever other powers a priest might learn, she knew why she had come to Northshire.

She had come to help.

By evening, Brinn had reached her fourth level of experience.

She had helped drive back wolves, fought kobolds, recovered books, completed errands for the abbey, and made herself useful to the people of Northshire.

But it was the library that drew her back.

Brother Paxton still had work to do, and Brinn had not forgotten him.

She approached the brother and spoke in the straightforward manner Maud had encouraged.

"can I help you Brother Paxton?"

It was a small question.

No grand declaration of faith. No promise to defend Stormwind against every enemy. No speech about destiny.

Just an offer of assistance.

And perhaps that was fitting.

The girl who had fled Lordaeron with her mother's hand upon her collar had spent five years learning that the world was full of wounds no single person could close.

She had also learned that this was no excuse to leave them unattended.

The abbey's library offered a quieter place to end the day.

Her bandages were packed. Her knowledge had grown. Her empty jar remained empty.

And tomorrow, there would be more work.

The Light willing, she would be there to do it.

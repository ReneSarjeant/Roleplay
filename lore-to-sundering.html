Warcraft Lore to Year -10,000

The night elf world, the Well of Eternity, the War of the Ancients, and the Great Sundering

A sourced reference for roleplay set in or after the ancient era. Distinguishes published Warcraft accounts from later novel and game expansions. Character interpretation is noted where used.

One continent and the Well

Ten thousand years before the First War, Azeroth held a single great continent called Kalimdor. At its heart stood the Well of Eternity, a vast lake of arcane power. The night elves (kaldorei) built their civilization around it. Their capital, Zin-Azshari, rose on its shores.

A privileged caste of sorcerers, the Highborne, studied the Well and learned to draw on its energies. Queen Azshara and her closest followers pushed these studies further, seeking greater power and refinement. Their work sent ripples of magic into the Twisting Nether.*1

The Highborne and the coming of the Legion

Those ripples drew the attention of Sargeras, the fallen titan who commanded the Burning Legion. Through the Highborne advisor Xavius, Sargeras reached Queen Azshara. He offered unimaginable power and a remade world. Azshara and most of the Highborne accepted. They opened a portal within the Well itself so the Legion could enter Azeroth.

Demonic forces poured through the capital. Archimonde and Mannoroth led the invasion. The Highborne who remained loyal to Azshara aided the demons. Resistance formed among other night elves, joined by the Sisterhood of Elune, the Moon Guard, some dissenting Highborne, the dragonflights, and forest allies such as the Ancient Guardians.*2

The War of the Ancients

The conflict that followed is known as the War of the Ancients. Malfurion Stormrage, already learning the ways of druidism from Cenarius, became a central figure in the resistance alongside Tyrande Whisperwind and, for a time, his brother Illidan. Lord Kur’talos Ravencrest and later Jarod Shadowsong led the night elf forces. The dragons entered the war, though the black Aspect Neltharion would later turn the Dragon Soul against both sides under the influence of the Old Gods.

The resistance fought to close the portal before Sargeras himself could step through. In the final struggle, the Highborne’s work was disrupted. An unstable vortex formed in the Well. Sargeras and the bulk of the Legion were hurled back into the Twisting Nether.*3

The Great Sundering

The destruction of the Well did not end cleanly. The implosion shattered roughly eighty percent of ancient Kalimdor’s landmass. Earthquakes tore the crust. The seas rushed in to fill the wound. What had been one continent became the separate landmasses known later as Kalimdor, the Eastern Kingdoms, Northrend, and Pandaria, along with many islands. At the site of the Well a permanent, raging whirlpool remained: the Maelstrom.

Millions died in the cataclysm. Queen Azshara and her loyal Highborne were drawn into the depths, where they were transformed into the naga. The surviving night elves withdrew to the slopes of Mount Hyjal. There the World Tree Nordrassil would later be planted, and a new Well of Eternity formed beneath it. The Long Vigil began.*4

What a survivor would have known

A night elf born in the Hyjal forests before the war, such as Dariand Nightwing, would have lived in a world that was still one continent. The Well still stood. The Highborne’s work was already changing the sky and the ground. When the Sundering came, the ground moved, the sky changed, and people known the day before were separated by water that had not existed. The exact words a survivor uses for those hours remain personal. The sources establish the scale of the breaking; they do not dictate every private memory.*5

After the breaking

The era that follows the Sundering lies outside this page. The night elves’ withdrawal, the planting of Nordrassil, the later exile of the remaining Highborne who would become the high elves, and the founding of the Skyborne refuge all belong to later centuries. This account stops at the moment the world split.

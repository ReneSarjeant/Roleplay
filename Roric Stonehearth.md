# Roric Stonehearth

Ironforge dwarf. Airfield mechanic turned Knight of the Silver Hand. 187 in year 25. Spent most of his years outdoors at the Ironforge Airfield keeping the machines flying. His nephew Brenn did not come back from a caravan run. The strip he kept was not wide enough.

## The Airfield

Roric Stonehearth spent most of his long life on the landing strips.

He was not a warrior by trade. He worked as ground crew and mechanic at the Ironforge Airfield on the northeast side of the mountain—the high valley where the wind cut and the snow never fully left the ground. He cleared the strips after storms, braced damaged frames, and helped the gnome pilots get their gyrocopters and bombers back in the air after hard landings or frost damage. Every dwarf in Khaz Modan grows up knowing how to swing something heavy if the door needs holding, but Roric had always preferred keeping the machines and the people who flew them in one piece. The work suited him. The mountain left its mark on his skin. The pilots and mountaineers knew his name.

His nephew Brenn was different. Younger, quicker, full of the restless energy Roric had mostly set aside. Brenn took work with caravans running supplies between Ironforge and the human kingdoms. He talked about the roads, the open country, the chance to see something beyond the mountain. Roric grumbled the way uncles do—said the work was dangerous, said a dwarf with sense stayed where the metal was solid and the strips were clear—and still slipped him an extra coin and a good knife before every run. He told himself it was just practical. Brenn never argued about it.

## On the Airfield (Reference)

The Ironforge Airfield was already a working strip and workshop years before Gnomeregan fell. The dwarves and gnomes had been neighbors and allies long before the trogg invasion and the irradiation of the city. Ironforge’s mountain is high and hard to reach by ground in bad weather; the dwarves needed a practical way to move light cargo, scouts, and messages without relying only on the lower roads or gryphons. The gnomes already had flying machines and the skill to improve them. The high valley on the northeast side of the mountain was a natural place for a strip: relatively flat for Dun Morogh, close enough to the capital for servicing, and workable once the snow was cleared. It was never a full military base in the early years—just a place where gnome pilots could test and maintain their machines and where dwarven ground crews kept the strips clear and handled frost damage. When the Second War came, and later when the Alliance needed more air capability, the strip was already there to expand. After Gnomeregan was lost it simply became more important as one of the remaining places the surviving gnome engineers could still work outdoors. The location itself was older than the disaster.

## The Caravan

The caravan never came back from one of those runs. Word reached the airfield that orcs had hit it hard in the foothills. They had not stopped at the supplies. Brenn did not make it. The news arrived on a quiet afternoon while Roric was checking a damaged wing on the strip. He finished the shift. He closed up the workshop. He sat until the lights went low and the valley was empty.

He did not go looking for a fight the next morning. He went to the Hall of Mysteries.

## The Hall

He had heard the Light’s teachings for years—human allies, dwarven priests, the same three virtues repeated in the halls—but he had always figured it was for other people. The ones who felt called to banners and plate. Brenn’s death made the old idea of “looking after my own” feel too small. If the roads outside the mountain were not safe, then the people who walked them—and the ones still flying the machines—were not really safe either. The trainers did not turn him away. They had taken stout-hearted dwarves after the wars before. Roric was older than most of the squires. He was slower with the formal doctrine and quicker with a practical question than a pious one. He trained anyway.

He learned to wear the plate. He learned to call the Light when his hands were not steady. He learned that justice was measured by more than the size of the hammer. The weight of it was different from a bracing tool or a heavy frame, but not so different from the work of keeping a machine in the air. Some days it still sits strange. Most days the shoulders already knew the weather and the weight.

After he took up the plate he learned Mining and Blacksmithing. The armor needed the ore and the forge. He had already spent decades working metal on the strip. Fishing was already his. He still prefers the line when the work is done.

## The Light

He still walks the high trails and the airfield perimeter. Some nights he is back on the strip helping with a stubborn engine. Other nights the plate is packed and he answers when the Alliance calls. Brenn’s knife is still on the shelf in the workshop. Roric has not moved it. He does not talk about the caravan much. When the day is done he checks the locks, clears the last of the snow from the strip, and makes sure the machines he used to keep are not the only things that matter anymore.

The Light, as he understands it, has room for an old dwarf who decided the airfield and the roads were not wide enough. He does not preach a quota. He shows up. Grief still sits close, but the work is the same as it ever was: keep the people under your watch safe. He just had to learn that the watch was larger than he thought.

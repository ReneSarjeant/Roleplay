Selective Timeline Overview

After the Sundering to Year 25 ADP — Troll Wars, human kingdoms, the wars, the Scourge, and the Forsaken

A selective high-level chronology focused on the Eastern Kingdoms and events relevant to these characters. Not a complete history. Numbered references distinguish classic published Warcraft accounts from Forever material and character canon.

Dates and scope: This repository uses Year 25 after the first opening of the Dark Portal (ADP) as its roleplay present. Blizzard describes Forever as occupying the original WoW era in its own time bubble; its panel recap calls this an early “Year 1” period. The two labels should not be treated as identical calendar statements.[1]

After the Sundering to the rise of the Eastern Kingdoms

Highborne who continued to use arcane magic were exiled from night elf lands. Led by Dath’Remar Sunstrider, they sailed east, founded Quel’Thalas, and created the Sunwell from preserved Well of Eternity water.[2]

The Amani troll empire contested the high elves. The Troll Wars saw high elves and the rising human kingdom of Arathor (under Thoradin) defeat the Amani, establishing early human–elf contact and helping secure the Eastern Kingdoms.[3] Over the following millennia the human kingdoms of Arathor’s successor states (including Lordaeron, Stromgarde, and others) rose. Dwarves and gnomes awakened and founded Ironforge and Gnomeregan in Khaz Modan; the two peoples became long-standing neighbors and allies. The Ironforge Airfield was already a working strip for gnome machines and dwarven ground crews well before later crises.[4]

The First War (Year 0–~4/5 ADP)

The Dark Portal opened. Orcish forces under the Burning Legion’s influence invaded Azeroth. The kingdom of Stormwind fell after years of war; King Llane was assassinated. Survivors, including young Prince Varian and Anduin Lothar, fled north to Lordaeron.[5]

The Second War (~Year 4–20 ADP)

King Terenas of Lordaeron called the human kingdoms together. The Alliance of Lordaeron formed. After heavy fighting the Alliance defeated the Horde. Anduin Lothar fell at Blackrock Spire; the Dark Portal was destroyed. Many orcs were placed in internment camps. Air capability expanded as the Alliance’s needs grew; existing strips such as the Ironforge Airfield were already in place and could be enlarged.[5] Character note: Roric Stonehearth spent most of his long life as ground crew and mechanic at that airfield. The strip was older than the later loss of Gnomeregan.[6]

The Third War and the rise of the Scourge (~Year 20–25 ADP)

The necromancer Kel’Thuzad founded the Cult of the Damned and spread the Plague of Undeath through Lordaeron’s grain. Infected people died and rose as mindless Scourge. Prince Arthas Menethil investigated with Jaina Proudmoore. After confronting the plague’s agents he traveled to Northrend, took up the runeblade Frostmourne, and lost his soul. He returned, murdered his father King Terenas Menethil II, and the Scourge overran Capital City. Lordaeron fell.[7]

Character note: Sister Brinn Sarjeant was fifteen when the bells went wrong in the outer wards of Lordaeron’s capital. Her father Ham stayed to bar the cottage door; her mother Edith fled with her. She has not settled her father’s fate and will not say “Undercity.”[8]

Arthas led the Scourge into Quel’Thalas. Silvermoon’s defenders were defeated; Ranger-General Sylvanas Windrunner was slain and raised as a banshee. Arthas used the Sunwell to resurrect Kel’Thuzad as a lich, polluting the waters. The Scourge then assaulted Dalaran; Archimonde was summoned. Guided by Medivh, Thrall’s Horde, Jaina’s humans, and the night elves under Tyrande and Malfurion united at Mount Hyjal. Malfurion unleashed Nordrassil’s power against Archimonde. The demon was destroyed; Nordrassil burned and the night elves lost their immortality.[7]

After the Legion’s defeat at Hyjal, the Lich King’s control over the Scourge weakened. Sylvanas Windrunner regained her free will. She rallied other free-willed undead who had broken from the Scourge. These renegades called themselves the Forsaken. They took the royal catacombs beneath the ruined Capital City (work Arthas had begun) and established the Undercity as their refuge. Many Forsaken had been Lordaeron humans or high elves who remembered their former lives and the horrors they had committed while mindless. Sylvanas gave them a purpose and a home; the faction later joined the Horde.[9]

Concurrently, Gnomeregan came under attack by troggs that had emerged after dwarven excavations near Uldaman. For roughly five years the gnomes fought the invasion. On the advice of Sicco Thermaplugg, High Tinker Gelbin Mekkatorque ordered the lower city irradiated to stop the troggs. The radiation killed the majority of the gnome population as well and turned many survivors into leper gnomes. Thermaplugg seized control of the irradiated city. Survivors fled to Ironforge; Tinker Town became their refuge. The Ironforge Airfield grew more important as one of the remaining outdoor workplaces for surviving gnome engineers.[10]

Character notes: René Sarjeant (born Nimbel Cogspool) was a mid-wards shop-child when the tanks were vented. She fled the city as a refugee and arrived at the Ironforge tally under the name stitched on a Stormwind soldier’s blanket. In Year 25 she is just of age, living on a cot in Tinker Town; Gnomeregan remains lost. Roric’s airfield work predates the fall and continues in Year 25; his nephew Brenn was later killed in an orc caravan raid, after which Roric trained as a paladin of the Silver Hand at the Hall of Mysteries.[6][11]

Year 25 ADP — the roleplay present

The sheets place characters in a world shaped by the Third War: Lordaeron’s capital is ruined and occupied in its depths by the Forsaken; Quel’Thalas has suffered the Scourge invasion; the night elves have lost their immortality; Gnomeregan is irradiated and under Thermaplugg. Forever adds the Skyborne’s crisis and return within its original-era setting. “Year 25” is the repository’s roleplay date; Forever’s published materials describe an early period in its own time bubble rather than equating the two calendars.[1]
